## Business & Why It Exists

DoorDash (NASDAQ: DASH), founded by Tony Xu (Stanford, 2013), is the largest U.S. food-delivery and local-commerce marketplace — a three-sided platform linking consumers, merchants (restaurants, grocers, retailers), and delivery drivers ("Dashers"). The founding insight ([IPO letter, Dec 2020](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000119312520292381/d752207ds1.htm): *"transform the way local merchants do business and enrich the communities"*) was that local-commerce logistics was fragmented, expensive, and slow, and that a three-sided marketplace with an AI logistics engine could commoditize delivery — expanding TAM from ~$30B (food delivery) to >$1T (groceries, retail, alcohol, pharmacy, restaurant tech). Food delivery was always the wedge, never the end state, which frames Wolt (2022), Deliveroo (2025), SevenRooms (2025), and New Verticals as coherent extensions. 2025's $5.1B M&A burst reframed DASH from "U.S. delivery #1" to "global local-commerce platform"; the bull case requires that thesis to hold, the bear case is permanent-but-low-margin commodity delivery.

## §3 — Competitive Moat & Landscape

**Narrow** — three-sided network effects + scale dominance + subscription stickiness, but not switching-costs-and-IP; requires continuous reinvestment to defend.

| Source | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Three-sided network effect | Strong | 56M MAU + 590K merchants + 9M+ Dashers — largest U.S. base by wide margin |
| U.S. share dominance | Strong | 60–67% (3× Uber Eats, 6× Grubhub); *widened* 60%→67% in 2022–25 despite Uber One bundling |
| DashPass / Wolt+ / Deliveroo Plus | Mod–Strong | 35M subs (+59%); ~30% of US MAUs order outside restaurants; higher frequency, lower churn |
| AI logistics ("Deep Red") | Moderate | Proprietary ETA/routing/matching; multi-year data advantage |
| Advertising flywheel | Mod (growing) | Highest-margin layer; underappreciated runway |

**Vulnerabilities**: consumer multi-homing is the norm (subscription is the only real switching cost); Uber One bundling (46M subs rides+delivery) in urban cores; gig-worker regulatory tail (AB5-style federal reclassification would destroy unit economics, mitigated but not eliminated by the [CA Supreme Court Prop 22 ruling, Jul 2024](https://www.capradio.org/articles/2024/07/25/doordash-uber-and-other-gig-work-companies-can-classify-workers-as-contractors-ca-supreme-court-rules/)); commission caps (NYC 15%, SF 20% as precedents).

| U.S. platform | Share | Read |
|---|---|---|
| **DASH** | **60–67%** (#1, widening) | Narrow but durable; share widened despite Uber bundling |
| Uber Eats | ~22–25% (#2) | Bundling (Uber One) is real but fragments per category; weaker on grocery — primary structural threat |
| Grubhub | ~7–10% (#3, declining) | Just Eat-owned; losing share; not a strategic threat |
| Instacart (CART) | grocery ~50%+ | Direct overlap on New Verticals; threatens DASH grocery margin recovery |
| Amazon Fresh / Whole Foods | n/d | Vertically-integrated; macro threat to grocery vertical, not a marketplace rival |

**International** (via Wolt + Deliveroo): Wolt #1 across Northern Europe (Finland/Denmark/Sweden/Germany; strong); Deliveroo #1/#2 in UK+Ireland (duopolistic vs. Just Eat, ~30% share defended) and Middle East (UAE/Kuwait/Qatar vs. Talabat/Careem); presence in Western Europe (vs. Just Eat, Uber Eats) and Singapore (sub-scale vs. foodpanda/Grab). Absent from LatAm (iFood, Rappi) and China/Korea/SE Asia ex-SG (Meituan, Coupang Eats, Grab).

**How DASH differs**: (1) scale-driven Dasher density (9M+) yields lowest delivery times, compounding consumer choice + full-rate merchant commission — Uber can match single cities, not cross-category scale (share *widened* 60%→67% despite Uber One); (2) 35M consolidated subs = largest delivery base outside Amazon Prime; (3) Wolt-precedent integration playbook (Wolt 2022 → profitable by FY2025) de-risks Deliveroo vs. Uber's rocky Postmates integration; (4) vertical depth (food + grocery + retail + restaurant CRM simultaneously) where peers are single-lane. **Tail risk**: Instacart is the under-priced competitive threat — if it acquires/partners into restaurant delivery to build a 2-sided set, DASH's H2 2026 grocery-margin-recovery thesis is delayed; not in consensus models.

*Sources: Bloomberg Second Measure (US share); [Statista food delivery](https://www.statista.com/outlook/dmo/online-food-delivery/worldwide); [Deliveroo footprint](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/teamupdate).*

## §7 — Industry Macro

**TAM**: U.S. food delivery ~$120–150B; DASH at 60–67% share on $100B+ GOV approaches core-category saturation — growth must come from local-convenience expansion (grocery/retail/pharmacy/alcohol → $1T+) and international. The sector has consolidated to a DASH / Uber Eats duopoly (Grubhub distant third); unit economics improved dramatically (DASH went from −$3/order to slightly positive on scale), with Dasher cost-per-order the core variable expense and autonomous the long-dated solution. **Regulatory** — two live risks: (1) city/state commission caps (NYC 15%, SF 20%, potentially extending); (2) federal gig-worker reclassification (a W-2 conversion adds ~$3–5/delivery, not survivable at current take rates without volume-shrinking price hikes). **Consumer macro**: delivery is "nice-to-have," frequency elastic to real disposable income — COVID proved spikes, a deep recession would test base frequency. Net setup mixed.
