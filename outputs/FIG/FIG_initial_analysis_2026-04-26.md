## Business & Why It Exists

Figma (NYSE: FIG; Field & Wallace, 2012) rebuilt the design tool around **collaboration as the primary primitive** — browser-native real-time multiplayer (WebGL + operational transforms) that Adobe and Sketch couldn't match without ground-up rewrites — making design a team sport and capturing the design-team workflow. The collaboration thesis won decisively for traditional workflows; the forward question (§5) is whether design remains a discrete workflow as AI code-gen collapses the design-to-code pipeline. Figma's answer is to integrate AI (Make, AI credits, Weavee) and position itself as the **collaboration surface** for AI-native creation. **The Adobe inheritance**: the $20B Adobe acquisition was abandoned 2023-12-18 under UK CMA + EU + DOJ pressure; Adobe paid a **$1B termination fee** that funded the AI build-out and the path to IPO. Went public 2025-07-31 at $33, surged to a **$142.92 first-day peak** (~$55B mkt cap, ~52× P/S), then fell to $17.47 (−47% from IPO) under three compounding forces: staggered lock-ups, AI-disruption overhang, and sentiment whiplash off an unsustainable peak.

## §3 — Competitive Moat & Landscape

**Narrow but widening.** Strong product-led moat within the traditional design-team workflow; the *category itself* is being reshaped by AI code-gen, and Figma's moat-extension bet is to be the **collaboration surface** connecting humans + AI agents.

| Source | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Browser-native real-time multiplayer | **Strong** | Multi-year architectural lead; Adobe XD discontinued, Sketch never matched. The original moat |
| Enterprise penetration / network effects | **Strong** | ~95% Fortune 500 design teams; files + Dev Mode in the eng workflow = high switching cost |
| Dev Mode workflow lock-in | **Mod-Strong** | Handoff specs, code snippets, design tokens — extends moat into the engineering org |
| Brand / "design verb" | **Moderate** | Default tool reference among modern product teams |
| Multi-product platform (8 products) | **Mod (widening)** | 70%+ on 3+ products; newer products less proven than core |
| AI-native repositioning (Make) | **Mod (developing)** | Make >50% of $100K+ weekly by Q4 — Figma is *capturing* AI value |

| Competitor | Type / Position | Threat vector |
|---|---|---|
| **Adobe** (XD discontinued; Express+Firefly+Photoshop) | Public, $250B+ cap | Re-entry via AI generative tools + Creative Cloud bundle pricing |
| **Canva** | Private, ~$32B (2024); ~190M MAU; Affinity (2024) | Largest direct private competitor; non-designer/SMB TAM building toward enterprise |
| **Microsoft Designer + Loop** | Bundled in M365 | Free in M365 + Copilot — enterprise bundling pressure on per-seat pricing |
| **Cursor / v0 / Lovable / Stitch (Google)** | AI-native | **Existential** — bypasses the dedicated design-file workflow if the bear case plays out |
| **Sketch** | Private, niche | Eroding macOS-native legacy — not a serious threat |
| **Penpot** | Open-source, free | Long-tail price/lock-in pressure; not an enterprise threat |
| **Miro / Mural** | Whiteboard-collab | Direct FigJam competitors; mostly contained |

**How Figma differs**: defensibility is the *combined* collaboration substrate + dev-handoff workflow + Auto Layout/Variants tooling depth + cross-functional adoption (designers + PMs + engineers in one file) — no single rival combines all four. Adobe has brand/bundle but not the multiplayer substrate; Canva has scale but not enterprise design-system depth or handoff; AI-native tools have the new-creation paradigm but no incumbent collaboration graph. **Evidence it's holding**: $1M+ cohort +68% YoY, international +45%, 70%+ multi-product, Make >50% weekly (§1). **Tail risk**: Canva is the best-capitalized adjacent SMB threat; Google Stitch (AI-native, free, Workspace-bundled) is the most concerning for the handoff segment. If 2026–27 adoption shows enterprise teams routing *around* Figma rather than through Make, the bear case becomes the base case.

## §4 — Management & Leadership

**Dylan Field (Co-founder, CEO, 33)** — founder-driven, navigated the Adobe-deal collapse, IPO'd on his own terms. **Dual-class super-voting Class B preserves founder control**; alignment strong. **Evan Wallace (Co-founder)** stepped back from day-to-day in 2021. **Praveer Melwani (CFO)** — pre-IPO hire, SaaS finance background. **Mike Krieger (Anthropic CPO, ex-Instagram co-founder) resigned from the board 2026-04-14** — a modest-negative info signal (credible AI-product leader stepping off mid-pivot; could be Anthropic workload — monitor for further signals).

**Capital allocation**: no dividend, no buyback (recent IPO). The $1.7B war chest (IPO proceeds + Adobe $1B termination legacy) is reserved for AI compute, hiring, and opportunistic M&A — per the S-1 founder letter, *"Expect us to take big swings when we see a chance to invest in our platform or pursue M&A at scale."* Weavee (AI creative tooling) acquired 2025; the abandoned $20B Adobe deal would have been the largest design-industry transaction ever.

**RMC synthesis** (Pattern B; primary set = 2025-07 S-1 founder letter + Q3'25 + Q4'25 transcripts): the short arc is coherent and accelerating, not defensive. Field frames AI as **non-optional offense** — *"AI is the most important technology shift of our lifetimes so far"* (Q3'25); *"As AI gets better, Figma gets better. Speed becomes a compounding advantage"* (Q4'25) — and pre-committed in the S-1 to multi-year margin compression to fund it (*"AI spend will potentially be a drag on our efficiency for several years"*), so the FY26 ~8% op margin is on-strategy. The S-1 also pre-committed to *"bias towards long-term compounding (measured in decades) over quarterly share price appreciation,"* foreshadowing exactly the post-IPO drawdown (*"We don't control that number, we control the inputs"* — [Fortune, 2025-09](https://fortune.com/2025/09/25/dylan-field-figma-stock-share-price-volatility-ipo/)). Q3 (Make 30% weekly) → Q4 (>50%, NDR +5pp to 136%, $1M+ +68%) is progressively stronger empirical proof the bet converts in the high-end base; "big swings" language is matched by Weavee + AI-credit monetization.

## §7 — Industry Macro

**TAM**: traditional collaborative-design tools ~$5–8B, growing low-double-digit; Figma is dominant share-holder. AI-creation surfaces (Make, code-gen) potentially expand the category into a far larger productivity/creation TAM — but expose Figma to much larger competitors (OpenAI, Microsoft, Google). **SaaS multiple compression**: broad since 2021; even high-quality SaaS trades far below peak. FIG's ~6× FY26E EV/Rev sits below MNDY (~6–8×) and TEAM (~10×) despite faster growth — that gap is the asymmetry. **AI-credit monetization regime change** (March 2026): Figma now has both a per-seat lever and a per-AI-action consumption lever — could materially expand revenue per customer as Make deepens. **Lock-up dynamics** (FIG-specific): until the **~Aug 2026** final expiration, every print is also a supply event; post-August, supply pressure structurally lifts.
