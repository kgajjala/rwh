## Business & Why It Exists

Netflix is the world's largest subscription streaming service — **325M+ paid memberships** across 190+ countries — monetized as a tripod: (a) subscription tiers (Standard, Standard with Ads, Premium) funding a $20B-class annual content slate; (b) a fast-scaling advertising layer via the in-house **Netflix Ads Suite** + partner DSP integrations (Microsoft Monetize, Google DV360, The Trade Desk, Amazon DSP, Yahoo, AJA); (c) live programming — NFL Christmas (year 3 in 2026), WWE Raw, FIFA Women's World Cup 2027, World Baseball Classic, boxing (Joshua–Paul). Production hubs in 50+ countries make non-English originals (*Squid Game S2*, *Money Heist: Berlin*, *Lupin*, *KPop Demon Hunters*) ~30%+ of engagement hours. A **10-for-1 split** took effect Nov 17, 2025 — all per-share figures here are split-adjusted (pre-split ~$1,341 high = $134.10). The equity story has shifted from sub-count beat/miss volatility to durable cash compounding via the ad layer + operating leverage.

## §3 — Competitive Moat & Landscape

**Wide and Widening.** Sources, all rated Strong unless noted: content scale ($20B+ FY26 spend = ~3× any pure-streaming peer, 50+ production hubs) · recommendation algorithm (industry-best engagement-per-member) · Open Connect CDN (lowest cost-to-stream globally) · global brand (190+ countries) · advertising platform (Moderate→Strong — Netflix Ads Suite in-house, 4,000+ advertisers, multi-DSP) · live/sports flywheel (Moderate — NFL Xmas year 3, WWE Raw, FIFA WWC 2027, Joshua–Paul 33M AMA).

| Peer | 2025 Scale / Engagement | Threat to NFLX |
|---|---|---|
| **Disney+ (+Hulu, ESPN+)** | ~175M D+ · ~135M Hulu · ESPN+ ~25M | Moderate — bundle is real US-household share-shift risk |
| **Amazon Prime Video** | ~200M+ Prime access; ad-tier on by default | Moderate — ad-tier scale + bundling pressures NFLX ad CPMs |
| **Max (WBD)** | ~125M subs | Low–Moderate — financially constrained; **terminated NFLX licensing deal for $2.8B fee (Q1 26)** = walking back streaming-only strategy |
| **Apple TV+** | ~50M est. | Low — narrow library, not a churn driver |
| **Paramount+ / Peacock** | ~80M / ~40M | Low — sub-scale economics |
| **YouTube (GOOGL)** | #1 US CTV time ~12% (Nielsen Gauge) vs. NFLX ~8% | **High (different vector)** — wins teen/young-adult attention; caps NFLX ad-tier CPM ceiling |
| **TikTok / Meta Reels** | Mobile short-form | Moderate — engagement-share vector, not subscription |
| **iQIYI / Tencent / JioHotstar** | National-champion CN/IN | Low — geography-bounded; NFLX has no CN exposure |

**How NFLX differs**: (1) global production scale at unit-economics depth no peer matches — the marginal foreign-language hit (KPop Demon Hunters: 325M views, all-time #1 NFLX film) is funded from mostly-fixed cost; DIS+/Max stay US-heavy. (2) Algorithm + Open Connect are decade-deep technical moats — NFLX is consistently #2 in US CTV time (~8%) on the smallest library by hour vs. YouTube, implying highest engagement-per-content-hour. (3) Ad-tech in-housing in 2025 captures the take-rate previously lost to intermediaries — the mechanism making $1.5B→$3B→$7B+ plausible at high margin. **Tail risk = YouTube** (not Disney/Max/Prime): ~10× the daily teen watch hours of all SVOD combined; the fight is for the attention pool ad CPMs depend on, with live-sports + mobile-video as the explicit response. Other vulnerabilities: mature-market saturation (US/UK/NL — growth must come from APAC+LATAM and ARM/ad-tier), sports-rights cost inflation, FX on ~57% international revenue, and a largely-harvested password-sharing tail.

## §4 — Management & Leadership

**Ted Sarandos (Co-CEO since Jan 2023)** owns content — architect of the originals strategy (House of Cards, Stranger Things, Squid Game), best-in-class content executive. **Greg Peters (Co-CEO since Jan 2023)** owns product/tech/advertising/partnerships/gaming — built the ad-tier from Nov 2022 launch to $1.5B+ (2025) and drives the in-house ad-tech; the operating execution lead. **Spence Neumann (CFO, since 2019)** — conservative, consistent guide-vs-deliver; the FY26 31.5% margin commitment is the cleanest near-term proof. **Reed Hastings** — founder; stepped down from board March 2024, now narrowed Executive Chairman; succession structurally complete.

**Capital allocation**: ~$3.6B repurchased FY2025 (share count down ~2%/yr); **$13.6B remaining authorization** at Q1 26 (mechanical ~$5–6B/yr bid). No dividend. Net debt ~$11B, investment grade, no refi risk. M&A disciplined — game-studio tuck-ins (Night School, Boss Fight, Spry Fox) + Animal Logic (animation); no mega-deals. The **$2.8B WBD termination fee (Q1 26) is NFLX *receiving* payment from Warner Bros. Discovery** to terminate a content-licensing arrangement — i.e., NFLX got paid to walk away, demonstrating content-spend bargaining leverage.

**RMC synthesis** (Pattern B quarterly letters): Sarandos (Q4 25) reframed the moat from "streaming wars" (won) to *attention-economy* competition (ongoing), validating the live-sports + mobile-video pivot under continued cost discipline. The 4,000+ advertiser count (+70% YoY, Q1 26) is the cleanest leading indicator the ad-tech in-housing is working; content-amort-<-revenue (~10% vs. 12–14%) is the explicit margin mechanism; Joshua–Paul's 33M AMA (Q3 25) confirms live's superior one-night/high-CPM unit economics. **5-yr arc**: framework evolved from "add subscribers at any price" (pre-2022 sub-loss shock → ads tier announced) through the FY23 paid-sharing crackdown + Co-CEO transition and FY24 ad scaling (op margin to 27%) to the current "revenue + engagement + margin" model (FY25 in-house ad-tech, Hastings board exit, 10-for-1 split; FY26 WBD termination + advertiser +70%).

## §5 — Strategic Growth Initiatives

**Advertising scale-up to $7B+ by 2028** is *the* growth + margin lever — path $1.5B (2025) → ~$3B (2026 guide) → ~$5B (2027E) → $7B+ (2028E), driven by ad-tier sub growth, Netflix Ads Suite first-party serving uplift, DSP broadening (added Amazon DSP + AJA in 2025), and live/sports CPM premium. Supporting vectors: **live & sports** (NFL Christmas, WWE Raw, FIFA WWC 2027, WBC, boxing) for engagement + sign-up funnel; **mobile/vertical-video product** ([CNBC, April 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/search/?query=netflix+q1+2026)) as the explicit TikTok/Reels attention-share response (early-stage); **localized production** at 50+ countries (~30%+ of engagement); **ARM expansion** (Jan 2026 UCAN/UK hikes absorbed without churn lift); and gaming (modest, long-arc, not a near-term driver).

## §7 — Industry Macro

**Global SVOD TAM** ~$120B (2025) → ~$180B (2028E); NFLX at $45B is ~38% share — saturating in mature markets, gaining in APAC+LATAM. **CTV ad market** ~$30B (2025) → ~$45B (2028E): NFLX's $3B 2026 target = ~7% share entering, with the $7B+ 2028 bull = ~15% share, achievable if Ads Suite first-party serving captures full take-rate. **Attention economy** is the durability question — YouTube has ~10× the daily teen watch hours of all SVOD combined; live-sports + mobile vertical-video are the response. US household streaming subs stabilized at ~4.5/home (Antenna/MoffettNathanson 2025), so future ARPU comes from price + ad-tier mix, not new adds. **Macro sensitivity**: subscription is recession-resilient (low ARPU vs. discretionary entertainment); the ad layer is more cyclical (CPM = advertiser demand), so mix shift marginally raises cyclicality. **Regulatory**: rising EU/UK content quotas, levies, cultural-investment obligations — absorbed today; step-change risk material but low-probability.

**Verdict**: B+A+I Moderate, T Weak → **Moderate conviction**. Quality compounder at fair-to-slightly-rich price; defensible long-term hold, not a screaming buy — better entry on a flush to $80–85.
