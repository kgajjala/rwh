## Business & Why It Exists

World's largest specialty coffeehouse — **40,990 stores across 89 markets** at FY2025 close, ~$36B revenue, ~381,000 partners, 35.5M U.S. Rewards members ([Q4 FY25 release](https://investor.starbucks.com/news/financial-releases/news-details/2025/Starbucks-Reports-Q4-and-Full-Fiscal-Year-2025-Results/default.aspx)). Hybrid model: ~52% of pre-Boyu units company-operated (U.S./Canada/UK/EMEA), the rest licensed/franchised (international, airports) plus the perpetual-royalty [Nestlé Global Coffee Alliance](https://www.nestle.com/media/pressreleases/allpressreleases/global-coffee-alliance-starbucks-2018) CPG channel. The investment narrative since Sept 2024 is the **Brian Niccol "Back to Starbucks" turnaround**: simplify menu, restore the third-place experience, fix mobile-order throughput, re-invest barista hours, re-accelerate U.S. transactions — addressing the 2022–24 comp decline, experience erosion, and China share loss to Luckin. The April 2026 [Boyu JV close](https://about.starbucks.com/press/2026/starbucks-and-boyu-capital-finalize-joint-venture-to-accelerate-long-term-growth-in-china/) converts ~8,000 company-operated China stores into a licensed/royalty + 40%-equity vehicle (path to ~20K stores), trading reported revenue for margin uplift and removing the structural drag. Bull case: transactions hold positive and FY27 EPS rebuilds toward $3.00+; bear case: Q1 was promotional sugar, margin recovery fails, and the multiple compresses toward franchised QSR (~22–25× MCD/YUM).

## §3 — Competitive Moat & Landscape

**Wide** — durable brand, coffee-sourcing scale, U.S. real-estate density, Rewards data network. The moat narrowed during the 2022–24 throughput-degraded experience erosion; the Niccol thesis is that it is being re-sharpened, not rebuilt, with the China overhang now removed.

| Source | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Global brand | Strong | top-10 global QSR; ~$36B rev at ~13–17% normalized op margin |
| U.S. real-estate density | Strong | ~17K U.S. units; replication = years of capex + zoning |
| Rewards data + lock-in | Strong | 35.5M members; ~50%+ of U.S. tender; pricing-elasticity data |
| Coffee sourcing scale | Moderate | C.A.F.E. Practices, grower contracts, hedging |
| Nestlé alliance | Moderate | perpetual CPG royalty, ~$1B+ high-margin, no distribution capex |
| China brand IP (post-Boyu) | New/Moderate | $13B-EV-validated royalty stream without operating drag |

**Vulnerabilities**: throughput vs. third-place tension (mobile-order surge degraded experience + productivity 2022–24); pricing-power limits (2022–24 price hikes lifted ticket but eroded transactions); labor (Workers United unionization → structurally higher post-2025 labor cost); Niccol key-person dependency.

## §4 — Management & Leadership

**Brian Niccol (CEO since Sept 9, 2024)** — ex-Chipotle, where he engineered the 2018–24 re-rating from ~$250 to ~$3,000+. "Back to Starbucks" five pillars: menu simplification, restoring the third place, re-investing barista hours, equipment refresh (espresso + cold hardware for the ~75%-iced U.S. mix), and U.S. transaction re-acceleration — the metric the thesis turns on, with Q1 FY26 +3% the first hard validation. FY25 comp ~$31M (~$5M cash + ~$20M+ stock; PSU-heavy on op-margin + TSR), per [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-27/starbucks-reports-ceo-brian-niccol-earned-31-million-in-2025). **Cathy Smith (CFO since late 2024)** — capital-allocation discipline; executed the Boyu JV; the "held-for-sale" classification cut China monthly expenses ~$39M from December 2025 (relief flowing ahead of close).

**Capital allocation**: dividend raised every year since 2010 (Aristocrat-track, $2.48 annualized); buyback paused 2022 (Schultz return), reinstated slowly 2024–25 but diverted to remodels + labor, expected to resume if FY26 H2 margins recover; M&A modest historically — the Boyu JV ($13B EV; ~$4B cash) is the largest strategic transaction in years; heavy 2025–26 reinvestment is the "Niccol tax" depressing FY25 EPS.

*Pattern C (no standalone shareholder letter); commentary via DEF 14A / 10-K front-matter / earnings.* Strategy arc Sept 2024→Apr 2026: diagnose (throughput-degraded experience, declining transactions, China loss) → menu simplification + barista reinvest → restructuring (627 closures, $1B+) → first global comp +1% (Q4 FY25) → Boyu announced (Nov 2025) → first U.S. txn +3% (Q1 FY26) → Boyu close (Apr 2026) → Q2 print (Apr 28). The CMG playbook: front-load investment, accept margin compression, prove transaction acceleration, then rebuild margins on the higher base. Niccol anchored Q4 FY25 as *"a year into… our turnaround is taking hold"*; Q1 FY26 moved the proof-point to U.S. transactions — the metric most diagnostic of moat health and most resistant to promotional manipulation. **Insiders (90d)**: no notable open-market activity; Niccol comp is multi-year PSU/RSU.

## §7 — Industry Macro

**TAM**: specialty coffee ~$120B globally, growing MSD; SBUX captures ~25–30% of branded specialty. The U.S. category is mature — growth comes from frequency, daypart expansion (afternoon + cold), and food attach — a SBUX/Dunkin'/local-roaster oligopoly with SBUX the premium leader; drive-thru (smaller-footprint formats) remains a meaningful vector. **China** is a fragmented battleground where Luckin's app-and-discount model structurally pressured per-cup pricing (now a Boyu JV problem). **Labor**: U.S. minimum-wage trajectory + unionization have been ongoing margin headwinds since 2022 (wage growth moderating, unionization continuing at the margin). **Consumer macro**: coffee is recession-resilient but not recession-proof (2008–09 transaction declines); tariffs / sticky inflation / real-wage softness pressure the recovery cadence, and GLP-1 is a multi-year secular question for food-attach revenue (not yet quantified by management). Foot-traffic data suggests 2025–26 stabilization after post-pandemic normalization; coffee remains among the most-defended discretionary purchases.

- 🟡 **Holder — Hold**: do not add ahead of the print, do not exit. Entrants below $80 have meaningful margin of safety; those near $100 are riding the catalyst.
