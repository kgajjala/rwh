## Business & Why It Exists

Trex (NYSE: TREX) is the **largest U.S. manufacturer of wood-alternative composite decking and railing**, made from ~95% recycled content (post-industrial polyethylene film + reclaimed hardwood fiber). The brand is functionally synonymous with "composite decking" for U.S. consumers — the structural segment least suited to low-cost wood substitution, defended by a recycled-content cost flywheel newer entrants cannot cheaply replicate. Hybrid channel: **Home Depot + Lowe's big-box** (~50% of sales, brand-defining shelf) and a **6,700+ specialty pro-dealer network** (~50%, growing toward 8,000+). Vertically integrated at Winchester, VA and the new Little Rock, AR plant (ramping through 2026; ~100bps near-term GM headwind via D&A, lower per-unit cost at full utilization).

FY2025 revenue ~$1.13B, adj. EPS $1.88. Outgoing CEO Bryan Fairbanks retires late April 2026; **COO Adam Zambanini becomes CEO** (internal continuity). The bull case needs the composite-vs-wood conversion (~50bps/yr against a 76% wood TAM) to compound while Little Rock absorbs into margin at the next cycle inflection; the bear case is a multi-year rate-stuck R&R recession with Little Rock stranded as fixed-cost drag.

## §3 — Competitive Moat & Landscape

**Narrow and widening.** Anchored on (a) brand (Trex = generic for "composite decking"), (b) recycled-content cost lead (~400M lbs/yr scrim feedstock pipeline, hard to replicate), (c) distribution density (big-box + 6,700 pros), (d) manufacturing scale. Widens because the composite-vs-wood category itself gains ~50bps/yr against a 76% wood-dominated TAM ([Q1'25 IR deck](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/trex-q1-2025-presentation-slides-market-leader-targets-wood-conversion-for-growth-93CH-4097787)) — every 1% conversion = **~$80M incremental industry composite sales**, of which TREX captures 50–60%. Composite still **<25% of total decking material share** — long runway.

| Peer | Type | Position | Threat to TREX |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[AZEK](https://www.azekco.com/) / TimberTech** | PVC cap-stock composite | ~3–8% of $8B US composite TAM; #2 | Most credible peer; well-capitalized; aggressive in pro channel + adjacencies (Versatex, Ultralox). Closest on EV/EBITDA |
| **Fiberon** ([Fortune Brands](https://www.fbin.com/)) | Composite | Mid-single-digit share | Diversified parent; mid-tier; dealer-channel overlap |
| **MoistureShield** ([CRH](https://www.crh.com/)) | Composite | Smaller | Mid-tier; less brand reach |
| **Deckorators** ([UFPI](https://www.ufpi.com/)) | Composite + mineral | Smaller | Niche; Vault mineral-composite differentiator |
| **Pressure-treated lumber** | Wood incumbent | ~76% of decking TAM | The *thing being displaced*; price floor. 1% conversion ≈ +$80M industry sales |

**Differentiator**: consumer brand + cost lead + dual distribution — few peers have all three. Through-cycle GM ~36–40% for TREX vs. AZEK ~31–35% (AZEK's virgin-resin PVC model is more ethylene/chlorine-cost-exposed). **Vulnerabilities / tail risk**: AZEK is the only peer with the balance sheet to escalate share-of-shelf battles (Fiberon second concern); railing is more competitive than decking and TREX's railing share is sub-leading; big-box concentration gives HD/LOW pricing leverage (mitigated by dealer growth); PE-film feedstock is a single-input concentration. Wood substitution remains a larger long-term opportunity than peer share loss.

## §7 — Industry Macro

**TAM**: US decking ~$8B/yr (composite + wood); [Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/us-composite-railing-decking-market-report) projects US composite railing & decking ~11% CAGR 2025–33 (composite alone ~13.3%). **Wood substitution**: PT lumber + cedar/hardwood = ~76% of TAM; each 1% conversion ≈ ~$80M industry composite sales / $40–48M to TREX; conversion ~50bps/yr historically. **Housing R&R cycle**: ~$500B US R&R spend (2024); LIRA implies modest deceleration through mid-2026; decks are higher-beta discretionary than core R&R. **Rate sensitivity**: 30-yr mortgage ~6.5% → existing-home sales ~4.0M annualized (vs. 5.5M+ norm); each ~50bps cut adds ~5–8% turnover, decking following on a 6–12-month lag. **Replacement floor**: ~60M US residential decks, ~30M aged 15+ yrs. **Regulatory tailwind**: recycled-content / circular-economy mandates (CA SB 54, EU EPR) advantage TREX's vertical pipeline.
