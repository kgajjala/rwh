## Business & Why It Exists

UnitedHealth (NYSE: UNH) is the largest U.S. health insurer + healthcare-services company by revenue ($447.6B FY2025), built on a **vertical-integration thesis** structurally rare among public managed-care peers. Two reporting segments: **UnitedHealthcare** (~$344.9B, the insurance arm — employer commercial, individual, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, global; **49.8M medical members**) and **Optum** (~$270.6B incl. intersegment) = Optum Health (value-based-care clinics, ~90K employed/affiliated physicians), Optum Insight (analytics + Change Healthcare claims clearinghouse), and OptumRx (#2 U.S. PBM by scripts). The closed-loop differentiator vs. ELV/HUM/CI/CVS/CNC: one member can be insured by UHC, treated by an Optum physician, filled by OptumRx, with data through Optum Insight — capturing value at every step of the dollar. Revenue is ~99% U.S. In 2025 the thesis hit its hardest test on record — MLR spiked to 89.1%, CEO Witty resigned (May 13), **Stephen Hemsley returned**, FY25 guidance suspended, Optum Health adj. earnings collapsed $7.9B→$2.3B, and a DOJ antitrust investigation into the integrated structure opened. Q1 2026 was the first quantitative confirmation the recovery is on the planned glide-path.

## §3 — Competitive Moat & Landscape

**Wide and durable** — structurally differentiated by tri-leg vertical integration; currently constrained in expression, not in source. Sources: scale (49.8M members → underwriting + network leverage), the **tri-leg closed loop** (unique vs. peers' two-leg analogs), employer switching costs (2–3yr contracts + ERISA friction), the **Optum Insight data moat** (50M+ members × decades of claims/Rx/clinical data; Change Healthcare aggregates non-UHC data too), the ~90K-physician Optum care network (cross-payer contracting → rivals fund UNH), and 50-state regulatory licensure. Brand/consumer lock-in only Moderate (B2B-mediated; reputation impaired post-Thompson).

| Competitor | Scale | Read on moat / threat |
|---|---|---|
| **Elevance (ELV)** | ~$120B cap, ~47M members | Two-leg (insurer + Carelon); narrower, cleaner MLR (~87%) but less normalization upside |
| **CVS Health** | Aetna + Caremark + retail | Closest tri-leg analog, but mid-tier PBM + uneven Aetna integration; deep discount (~10× FY26) |
| **Cigna (CI)** | Express Scripts + Evernorth | Two-leg PBM-heavy; divested MA in 2024 → less rate-cycle risk; cleanest MLR (~84%) |
| **Humana (HUM)** | MA-pure (~16M MA) | MA-concentration pain; deep margin loss now — UNH's diversification is the contrast |
| **Centene (CNC)** | Medicaid (~28M) | Medicaid-dominant, ~10× FY26 on rate-adequacy risk |
| **Molina (MOH)** | Medicaid-pure | Smaller, similar Medicaid risk to CNC |

PBM peer-set (OptumRx #2): CVS Caremark (#1 by lives), Express Scripts (#3, CI) — ~3 firms cover >80% of insured lives; stalled PBM legislation is a regulatory barrier. Care-delivery: hospital systems (HCA, THC, CYH) + sub-scale primary-care plays (Walgreens-VillageMD, Amazon-One Medical) vs. Optum's ~90K physicians. Analytics: Epic/Oracle-Cerner/McKesson — none has Change Healthcare's cross-payer claims flow. **How it differs (evidence)**: (1) tri-leg is *organic and structural*, not the later/smaller bolt-ons rivals attempted — Optum Health's pre-dislocation >$7.9B FY24 adj. earnings had no peer comparable; (2) cross-payer Optum fees mean rivals' revenue partly funds UNH; (3) CMS concentration is industry-shared, not UNH-specific — diversification cushioned the FY25 shock better than MA-pure HUM or Medicaid-pure CNC. **Tail risk**: DOJ vertical-integration action could *forcibly* unwind the moat (low prob, enormous severity) — UNH's main competitive risk is regulatory, not commercial (no peer can take share at margin today).

## §7 — Industry Macro

**MLR / utilization is the dominant variable** — FY25's 89.1% vs. historical 82–85% = ~400bps excess losses, driven (per 10-K MD&A + Q1 call) by post-COVID behavioral-health surge, MA age-up/acuity mix, CMS prior-auth changes raising approval rates, Medicaid redetermination (higher-acuity stayers), and GLP-1 step-up; 100bps = ~$4–5B EBIT (per §2). **MA CMS rate-setting** is the critical annual lever: FY25 ~flat/unfavorable, FY26 modestly improved, FY27 V28 risk-adjustment phase-in completes (the dominant forward risk); 1% favorable rate ≈ $1–2B EBIT on the MA book. **Medicaid**: post-COVID unwinding left higher-acuity enrollment; state rebids create periodic risk (UNH less exposed than CNC/MOH but losses concentrated). **Drug pricing**: IRA Part D OOP cap + Medicare negotiation bind in 2025–26, pushing PBMs toward transparent pass-through — OptumRx repositioning underway. **Provider consolidation**: hospital pricing power vs. payers, with Optum Health the natural hedge. **Rates** (secondary): the investment portfolio benefits from higher rates, partly offsetting MLR pressure.
