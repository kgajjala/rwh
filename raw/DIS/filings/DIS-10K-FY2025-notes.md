## Item 1 (Business) — Segment Structure
Disney reports three segments beginning FY2024 (carried into FY2025):
- **Entertainment** — linear networks (ABC, Disney Channel, FX, National Geographic), Direct-to-Consumer (Disney+, Hulu), and Content Sales/Licensing/Other (theatrical, home entertainment, stage plays).
- **Sports** — ESPN linear networks, ESPN+, ESPN International, Star India sports (through divestiture), and (new FY2025) the standalone ESPN app.
- **Experiences** — Parks & Experiences (domestic + international theme parks/resorts, Disney Cruise Line, Disney Vacation Club) and Consumer Products (merchandise licensing, games).

## Item 7 (MD&A) — FY2025 Full-Year Results
Source: [Q4/FY2025 earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1744489/000174448925000154/fy2025_q4xprxex991.htm) via WebSearch extraction; [CNBC Q4 2025 earnings coverage](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/13/disney-dis-earnings-q4-2025.html).

- **Total revenue**: $94.4 billion, up 3% from $91.4 billion in FY2024.
- **Total segment operating income**: $17.6 billion, up 12% from $15.6 billion in FY2024.
- **Diluted EPS (GAAP)**: $6.85, up from $2.72 in FY2024.
- **Adjusted (excluding certain items) EPS**: $5.93, up 19% from $4.97 in FY2024.
- **Q4 revenue**: $22.5 billion, roughly flat vs. Q4 FY2024.
- **Capital expenditures**: $8.0 billion, up sharply from $5.4 billion in FY2024 — driven by cruise ship fleet expansion (Disney Cruise Line new ships) and, to a lesser extent, new theme park attractions in Experiences.
- **Free cash flow**: ~$10.08 billion for FY2025 (source: [AlphaQuery FCF annual data](https://www.alphaquery.com/stock/DIS/fundamentals/annual/free-cash-flow), cross-referenced against press release commentary).
- **Net debt/EBITDA**: 1.9x at FY2025 year-end — lowest since 2018 (pre-Fox acquisition, pre-streaming buildout, pre-heightened Experiences capex).

### Q4 FY2025 Segment Detail
- **Entertainment**: Q4 operating income $691 million, down $376 million YoY — driven by theatrical slate comparisons (tough comp vs. prior-year quarter).
- **Sports**: Q4 operating income $911 million (one source) / $898 million (another source, ESPN-specific) — down modestly YoY (~$18M decline cited for one framing). ESPN Q4 revenue ~$4 billion, up 3%. Domestic ESPN operating income pressured by costs associated with the August 2025 launch of the standalone ESPN app plus higher programming costs (NBA, college sports rights).
- **Experiences**: record Q4 operating income $1.9 billion, up $219 million YoY.

### Direct-to-Consumer (Streaming) — FY2025 / Q4 Detail
Source: [AlphaStreet](https://news.alphastreet.com/disneys-dis-streaming-business-continues-its-momentum-in-q4-2025/), [TheWrap Q4 2025 earnings coverage](https://www.thewrap.com/disney-earnings-q4-2025/).

- **Disney+**: 132 million subscribers (131.6M per one source), +3.8 million in the quarter.
- **Hulu**: 64.1 million subscribers.
- **Global average monthly ARPU**: $8.04.
  - Disney+ domestic ARPU: $8.09 (flat YoY).
  - Disney+ international ARPU: $8.00 (+4% YoY).
  - Hulu SVOD-only ARPU: $12.20 (down).
  - Hulu + Live TV ARPU: $100.02 (down).
- **DTC operating income**: $352 million in Q4, up $99 million (+39%) YoY, driven by subscriber growth and ARPU gains.
- **IMPORTANT DISCLOSURE CHANGE**: Q4 FY2025 was the **last quarter Disney disclosed DTC subscriber counts and ARPU** — the company is following Netflix in discontinuing this disclosure going forward. This is material for future Section 9 catalyst tracking (loss of a previously standard KPI).

### Experiences (Parks) — FY2025 Detail
Source: [WDWInfo](https://www.wdwinfo.com/news-stories/disney/disney-parks-post-record-10b-income-in-2025/), [BlogMickey](https://blogmickey.com/2025/11/disney-parks-2025-attendance-down-slightly-guest-spending-increases/), [ThatParkPlace](https://thatparkplace.com/disney-parks-attendance-falls-in-2025-while-prices-keep-rising-what-disneys-new-numbers-reveal/).
- Domestic parks attendance: down ~1% YoY.
- International parks attendance: up ~1% YoY.
- Domestic per-guest spending: +5% YoY (up from +3% in FY2024) — higher ticket prices, food/beverage, merchandise.
- International per-capita spending: +2% YoY (down from +4% in FY2024 pace).
- Resort occupancy (per Q3 FY2025 data point): domestic (WDW/Disneyland) up from 83% to 86%; international up from 82% to 87%.
- Record full-year Experiences operating income of $10.0 billion despite flat-to-down attendance — thesis: monetization/pricing power more than offsetting attendance softness.

## Item 1A (Risk Factors) — FY2025 10-K
Extraction limited by inability to fetch full-text; below are risks confirmed via targeted search with partial verbatim language. **Full verbatim risk-factor text could not be independently confirmed for all items — flagged individually below.**

1. **AI / technological disruption to IP-driven business model.** Confirmed near-verbatim via search: *"Regulations governing new technological developments, such as developments in artificial intelligence (AI), including generative AI and large language model tools, remain unsettled, and these developments may affect aspects of [Disney's] existing business models, including revenue streams for the use of their IP, how they create their entertainment offerings and the competition they face."* This is a strong candidate for [NEW or substantially expanded in FY2025 10-K] — AI risk language has escalated year over year across the industry; needs FY2024 10-K side-by-side to confirm delta (see FY2024 notes file).
2. **Macroeconomic sensitivity — Experiences demand.** Paraphrased/partial verbatim: *"slowdowns in economic activity and/or inflationary conditions"* can result in *"lower attendance and spending"* at parks; *"actual or perceived declines in economic conditions typically have impacts across our businesses, including, among others, lower attendance and spending at our parks and experiences businesses."*
3. **Discount rate / pension obligation sensitivity.** Company disclosed a pension discount-rate increase to 5.45% from 5.06%, which increased pension obligations by $2.1 billion at FY2025 year-end (per [SignalX extraction](https://signal-x.app/stock/walt-disney-company-the/10-k/2025)) — a quantified market-risk-adjacent disclosure, likely in Item 7A or the pension footnote rather than Item 1A proper; flagged here as a material balance-sheet-affecting item.
4. **Sports media rights cost escalation.** Not fully verbatim-confirmed as an Item 1A risk-factor bullet, but the NBA national TV rights deal beginning FY2026 carries ~$2.6 billion/year for the ESPN portion — a large step-up in fixed programming cost exposure (source: [Forbes, Feb 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/johncassillo/2026/02/03/rising-sports-media-rights-costs-create-long-term-disney-questions/)). Total produced/licensed content spend (incl. sports rights, excl. Star India) guided at ~$24 billion for FY2025.
5. **Consumer/public alignment risk on social and environmental positioning.** Disney's 10-K reportedly states that consumers' *"positions on Disney's environmental and social goals often differ widely and present risks to our reputation and brands"* (per [ThatParkPlace summary](https://thatparkplace.com/the-walt-disney-company-admits-that-consumers-positions-on-disneys-environmental-and-social-goals-often-differ-widely-and-present-risks-to-our-reputation-and-brands/) of the 10-K) — politically-charged content/brand risk, elevated for Disney specifically given the 2022–2024 Florida/Reedy Creek dispute history.
6. **Changing consumer tastes and preferences.** *"Misalignment with public and consumer tastes and preferences for entertainment, travel and consumer products, which impact demand for our entertainment offerings and products and the profitability of any of our businesses."*
7. **Litigation / legal contingencies — uncertain outcomes.** General disclosure that legal risk from ongoing litigation could materially affect results; estimates developed with outside counsel. Material specific litigation item: **$50 million settlement** in the YouTube TV / DirecTV Stream antitrust class action (carriage-bundling practices) — new multi-year distribution deal with YouTube TV reached November 15, 2025 includes ESPN Unlimited access and a shift toward genre-specific add-on tiers rather than base-package-only carriage (source: [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/cpi-posts/disney-reaches-50-million-settlement-in-streaming-tv-antitrust-case/), [OpenClassActions](https://openclassactions.com/settlements/disney-class-action-settlement.php)). Claims deadline Sept 8, 2026; final approval hearing Jan 14, 2027.
8. **Cable/satellite/telecom distribution consolidation.** Confirmed via search extraction: risk language addressing *"consolidation...in the cable, satellite and telecommunication distribution industry that may adversely affect [Disney's] ability to obtain and maintain contractual terms for distribution of programming services."*

## Item 7A (Market Risk)
- Pension discount rate: 5.45% (FY2025) vs. 5.06% (FY2024) — a rate increase (favorable to pension liability normally, but the search-extracted commentary states this *raised* pension obligations by $2.1B, which is counter to typical discount-rate mechanics; this figure needs verification — possibly reflects an asset-side or actuarial-assumption interaction not fully captured by search summarization).
- Interest rate hedging: as of the most recent confirmed data point (FY2023 10-Q sourcing), Disney used pay-floating interest rate swaps as fair-value hedges of fixed-rate borrowings, notional ~$13.5 billion (as of Dec 30, 2023 / Sept 30, 2023) — **FY2025-specific notional not confirmed; carry-forward figure, needs update.**
- FX risk: general disclosure regarding reduced ability to hedge currency exposure or repatriate revenue in certain international markets given local economic/political conditions.
