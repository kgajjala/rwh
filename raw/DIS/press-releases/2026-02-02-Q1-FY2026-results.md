## Major One-Time Item: YouTube TV Carriage Dispute / Blackout

- ESPN suite of networks (plus ABC and others) went dark on YouTube TV starting late Oct 30, 2025 (just before this quarter's period) — a **15-day blackout**.
- **Quantified hit**: **-$110 million to ESPN's (Sports segment) operating income** for the quarter — works out to roughly $7+ million/day just for ESPN.
- Dispute was over price hikes Disney was seeking; resolved via new multi-year carriage deal, ending the blackout.
- This is the primary driver of the Sports segment's -23% YoY operating income decline this quarter (along with higher programming/production costs).
- [Source](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/espn-110-million-lost-income-youtube-tv-blackout-1236648725/) and [Source](https://mickeyblog.com/2026/02/02/disney-paid-a-hefty-price-for-the-youtube-tv-blackout/)

## Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) / Streaming — First Quarter WITHOUT Subscriber Disclosure

- **CONFIRMED DATA GAP**: This is the **first quarter Disney did NOT disclose Disney+, Hulu, or Hulu+Live TV subscriber counts**, consistent with the disclosure-change flagged at the Q3 FY2025 call. Investors must track streaming progress via revenue/profit only going forward.
- **Entertainment SVOD (Disney+, Hulu, Hotstar) revenue**: $5.35 billion (also cited as "Disney+ and Hulu" $5.3 billion), **+11% YoY**.
- **SVOD operating income**: **$450 million, +72% YoY** (up substantially from a year ago).
- **SVOD operating margin**: ~8.4%.
- Management **reaffirmed FY2026 full-year target of 10% SVOD operating margin**.

## ESPN / NFL Deal Closing

- The **NFL transaction closed January 31, 2026** — Disney/ESPN acquired NFL Network and other NFL media assets (including linear rights to NFL RedZone); NFL took a **10% equity stake in ESPN** as part of the deal structure.
- Purpose: bolster **ESPN Unlimited** (the standalone flagship DTC sports service) — ESPN gained ability to sell/bundle "NFL+ Premium" (NFL Network + NFL RedZone) to ESPN DTC subscribers.
- Management characterized ESPN Unlimited adoption/engagement as "still early days" but "pleased" with what they'd seen, including uptake of new app features.
- [Source](https://thewaltdisneycompany.com/news/disney-q1-earnings-2026/) and [Source](https://www.streamtvinsider.com/content/espn-exchanges-10-equity-nfl-network-disney-molds-streaming-vision)

## Parks & Experiences Detail

- Domestic parks attendance **+1% YoY** — benefited from comparison to prior-year quarter's Hurricane Milton disruption.
- Per capita guest spending **+4% YoY**.
- Domestic Parks & Experiences operating income **+8% YoY**.
- Continued softness noted in international visitation (though this was described as improving/rebounding in later commentary — Shanghai Disneyland attendance +5.0% YoY to 14.7M, Hong Kong Disneyland +3.3% YoY to 7.9M, Disneyland Paris -1.8% YoY).

## Studio / Content

- **Zootopia 2** (released November 2025): $1.9 billion global box office — one of Disney's biggest animated hits ever, surpassing Avatar: Fire and Ash at the box office. Zootopia franchise surpassed 1 billion hours streamed on Disney+.
- **Avatar: Fire and Ash** (released December 2025): also surpassed $1 billion global box office.
- Both were called out as powering the quarter's results.
- Forward slate mentioned for calendar 2026: The Devil Wears Prada 2 (20th Century), The Mandalorian and Grogu (Lucasfilm), Toy Story 5 (Pixar), live-action Moana (Disney), Avengers: Doomsday (Marvel).
- AI/Sora: Iger discussed a deal with OpenAI for Sora-generated video content, wanting to bring short-form/user-generated content features to Disney+ similar to what ESPN did on its new app.
- [Source](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/feb/2/zootopia-2-avatar-sequel-power-disney-strong-quarter/) and [Source](https://collider.com/zootopia-2-overtakes-avatar-3-fire-and-ash-box-office-1-7-billion/)

## CEO Succession Context (IMPORTANT — Timing Note)

- This call (Feb 2, 2026) was Bob Iger's **last earnings call as CEO** — Disney announced Josh D'Amaro as his successor the **very next day, Feb 3, 2026** (effective March 18, 2026), so the succession announcement itself was NOT discussed live on this call.
- However, analysts probed succession-adjacent questions: one analyst asked Iger what areas he sees his successor being able to "jump start" for long-term growth. **Iger's response**: the company is "in better shape now than three years ago."
- Iger, discussing Parks vs. Entertainment: said Disney now has "healthy competition" between its parks and entertainment divisions over "which is going to essentially prevail as the number one driver of profitability for the company." On Parks specifically: "As I look ahead, I actually am very, very bullish on that business and its ability to grow," and "the footprint of the business today... has never been more broad or more diverse."
