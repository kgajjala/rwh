=== HEADLINE ===
"We need to grow faster and take more market share in DIY taxes."

=== ON THE CUSTOMER-GROWTH SLOWDOWN (the key off-script framing) ===
Goodarzi attributed the slowdown to Intuit's OWN allocation of focus rather than to
competitive loss: the company shifted attention to building the agentic platform and its
three Big Bets (Assisted Tax, Money, Mid-market), which caused the deceleration in
new-customer acquisition in DIY tax and at the low end of the business segment. His claim
is that Intuit now has a platform with more services to drive growth, and is "doubling
down on new-to-franchise acquisition."

He repeated the accountability line from the call: personally dissatisfied with DIY tax
and low-end business performance, areas where Intuit historically grew customers at
double-digit rates.

=== ON PRICING ===
Consistent with the call: lost quality DIY customers to low-cost providers; price is the
number one reason customers leave TurboTax; Intuit will deliberately lower FY2027 DIY
prices to acquire and retain customers.

=== ON STRATEGY ===
"Resetting expectations to play offense" — scaling the Big Bets while rebuilding the
new-customer funnel.

=== ANALYST NOTE (agent) ===
The self-diagnosis is the crux of the thesis: if the customer-growth stall is an internal
attention-allocation problem it is fixable; if it is competitive displacement by AI-native
and low-cost rivals (the BofA and JPMorgan read) it is structural. Management's answer is
unambiguous but is a claim to hold them to, not evidence. Falsifiable at Q1 FY27
(~late Nov 2026) via QuickBooks paying-customer growth, and at Q3 FY27 (~May 2027) via
TurboTax units.
