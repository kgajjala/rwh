--- Harvey Jones, Chairman ---
On November 12, our Board of Directors announced that after disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct, standards, and cultural norms, Jensen Huang voluntarily resigned his positions as President, CEO, and Board Member. The NVIDIA board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO. The board appointed Colette Kress, NVIDIA's longtime and well-respected CFO, as Interim CEO.

In addition, the board announced it was initiating a search for a permanent Chief Executive, and we have since retained Heidrick & Struggles to lead the search. ... This morning, the company announced that our board has established a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee that will include a select group of our independent board directors, senior external advisors, and legal counsel. This committee will enhance our engagement with the U.S. government, including all relevant agencies. We will not be addressing or taking questions on today's call regarding the circumstances of Jensen's resignation and the ongoing investigation.

--- Colette Kress, prepared remarks ---
Vera Rubin is off to an extraordinary start. In its first quarter of production shipments, the Rubin platform generated nearly $20 billion in revenue, making it the fastest product ramp in NVIDIA's history.

This combined with increased support from our supply base, we are raising our fiscal year 2028 outlook. We expect revenue to grow approximately 75% year-over-year, up from 70%, and off what is now a higher fiscal 2027 base. Our outlook considers data center readiness, the strength and durability of end customer demand, and our customers' access to capital. Q3 data center revenue increased 21% quarter-over-quarter to $108 billion, with balanced growth across hyperscale and AI cloud, industrial, and enterprise subsegments. Hyperscale revenue of $60 billion grew 23% sequentially, driven by the Vera Rubin ramp and sustained strength in Grace Blackwell. Our hyperscale customers' cloud revenue growth accelerated for the sixth consecutive quarter. Cloud industry backlog now sits at $2.5 trillion, up from $2 trillion just three months ago.

We expect the major frontier labs to contribute approximately 15% of our data center revenue this year.

Token prices have fallen nearly 60% since May, accelerating token usage and demand for compute. As a result, GPU rental prices are rising across generations. Hopper GPUs have recontracted at up to twice their original price under the multi-year agreements, while the B300 rates have risen more than 60% since their introduction.

We continue to see very strong demand for Vera and expect total CPU revenue to more than double in fiscal year 2028. AI Clouds, Industrial, and Enterprise, or ACIE, revenue of $47 billion increased 18% sequentially and nearly tripled year-over-year. Sequential growth was driven by Vera Rubin shipments to top leading neocloud customers and the start of our frontier labs building their own data centers. ... We have supported the development of this ecosystem, and we will continue to be disciplined and selective in our support.

Q3 sales of licensed Hopper data center products to China was $2.4 billion. Our guidance does not have any licensed Hopper data center products included, and we are no longer shipping licensed Hopper data center products to China.

In Q3, our purchase commitments increased to $390 billion, reflecting our confidence in the long-term sustainability of demand for NVIDIA infrastructure. ... Commitments associated with our neocloud agreements totaled $32 billion at the end of Q3. ... Total guarantees were $118 billion at the end of Q3, including the $105 billion in credit support for land, power, and shell capacity for the PORTS-Pike campus we discussed last quarter. ... SB Energy plans are on track, with capacity expected to come online in phases beginning in calendar 2028.

As we advance this work, we will remain thoughtful in how we participate and disciplined in how we commit capital. ... As the market matures and additional sources of capital become available, we are rigorously evaluating future investments, commitments, and guarantees.

Our networking franchise continues to scale, with Q3 revenue exceeding $21 billion. ... We remain on track to launch Vera Rubin Ultra next year and Feynman in 2028.

In Q3, we returned a record $28 billion to shareholders, $22 billion through share repurchases, and $6 billion in cash dividends. We have returned 65% of free cash flow on a year-to-date basis. Going forward, we intend to return all excess free cash flow, net of strategic uses, to shareholders during this last quarter and going forward. ... We continue to plan to execute our buyback authorization by the end of fiscal 2028.

Total revenue is expected to be $130 billion, plus or minus 3%. ... New export controls were released by the U.S. Commerce Department this week. However, with demand far exceeding supply, we expect our performance to remain strong. ... We expect Q4 to mark the bottom of gross margins before improving to the 72%-73% range in fiscal year 2028.

Additionally, we did look at an assessment in terms of the current export controls, and we are still going to spend some time studying those to see the different pieces of it.
