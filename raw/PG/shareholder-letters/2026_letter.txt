over $10 billion in dividends and $5 billion in share

dividend increase, and the 136th consecutive year
P&G has paid a dividend.
In summary, ﬁscal 2026 was another year of sales 
and earnings growth and strong cash return to 
shareowners. Despite the volatile geopolitical, 
macroeconomic and consumer dynamics we 
faced — headwinds not anticipated at the start of
the ﬁscal year — we delivered within our going-in 
guidance ranges across all key ﬁnancial metrics.
There is much to build on in the year ahead with 
broad-based growth across all regions and with
nine of 10 categories growing or holding organic
sales. We also ﬁnished the year holding global share, 
with trends improving in the second semester.

progress we expect moving forward, and we are 
committed to improving results semester by
semester and year by year.

We met our core objectives for ﬁscal year 2026 despite 
unexpected headwinds. We managed through a very 
volatile environment to deliver another year of organic 
sales and core earnings-per-share growth within our 
initial guidance ranges. We built plans to return to 
consistent growth across all categories and regions, 
and we stabilized global market share. Importantly, 
we continued our long-standing track record of 
returning cash to you, our shareowners.
Organic sales grew more than 1%. Core earnings per 
share grew 1%. On a currency neutral basis, core 
earnings per share were in-line with the prior year. 
Adjusted free cash ﬂow productivity was 100%.
Over the course of the year, we sequentially improved 
top-line results — moving from up one point in the
ﬁrst half of the year to two points in the second half.
We also saw improvement in market share in the
second half despite some softening in underlying 
market growth as inﬂation increased. Positive trends
to build on in the year ahead.
Growth was broad-based across categories
and regions.
Nine of 10 product categories held or grew organic 
sales for the year. Hair Care and Skin & Personal Care 
each grew mid-single digits. Personal Health Care, 
Baby Care, Home Care, Fabric Care, Feminine Care, 
Grooming and Oral Care were each in-line to up
low single digits. Family Care was down for the year.
All regions held or grew organic sales.
Focus market organic sales were up 1% for the year, 
with North America and Europe Focus Markets each 
up modestly. Greater China organic sales were up 
4% for the year.
Enterprise markets were up 4%, led by Latin America 
with 6% organic sales growth.
E-commerce sales increased 6%, now representing 
20% of Company total sales.
Twenty-six of our top 50 category/country combinations 
held or grew share for the ﬁscal year. Five of 10 product 
categories held or grew share globally. In aggregate, 
global value and volume share trends improved in the 
back-half, exiting the year roughly ﬂat.

As we observe changes in the world around us,
we see many opportunities we want to pursue and 
risks we will mitigate. There are three important 
changes to highlight.
The ﬁrst change is media fragmentation. In today’s 
fragmented media landscape, it is much more difﬁcult 
to capture consumers’ attention and educate them 
about the beneﬁts of our brands. People have more 
sources of information, more sources of inﬂuence
and more ways to engage — or disengage — than ever 
before. This gives P&G an opportunity to lead another 
evolution of brand communication — building 
awareness, trust and preference through messages 
that are more timely, more relevant and more directly 
connected to the superior beneﬁts our brands deliver.
The second change is in the retail landscape. 
Consumers are shopping differently. Consider
new shopping methods such as agentic commerce,
quick commerce and social commerce. In the
U.S., channels like e-commerce, d-commerce and
club are growing exponentially, while traditional 
formats are consolidating. Additionally, retailers are
becoming media platforms, and media platforms
are becoming retailers. We believe these shifts

The third change is inﬂation, which has grown
steadily over the last ﬁve years and is affecting energy, 
food, healthcare and many other areas of household 
spending. This has placed signiﬁcant pressure on 
consumers around the world. Even consumers with 
higher disposable income are becoming more 
disciplined and thoughtful about how they spend —  
where they choose to trade up and what they expect
in return for every purchase. This is an opportunity
to better serve consumers with offerings at the right 
sizes and price points, while continuing to deliver the 
performance, quality and value consumers expect
from P&G brands across every value tier.
In summary, the consumer path to purchase is 
changing daily, is non-linear and ﬁlled with millions
of possible distractions. To adjust for and beneﬁt from 
these shifts in the external landscape, we are making 
four interventions to strengthen how we execute our 
strategy to win with consumers and accelerate growth. 
Putting the Consumer First
The ﬁrst intervention is to have a deeper, more 
complete connection with consumers. Nothing 
matters more than putting the consumer ﬁrst in 
everything we do. “Consumer Is Boss” is not a slogan;
it is a belief that has shaped P&G for generations.
Our opportunity now is to apply this belief with
greater consistency and precision — end to end. 
A consumer-ﬁrst mindset means that each decision
we make — from product development to packaging
to communication to retail strategy — must be 
meticulously crafted based on how it will be received 
by the consumer. Our goal is to deliver a superior 
consumer experience at every single touchpoint.
This requires having a profound, empathetic 
understanding of the people we serve. It means
seeing the world through their eyes and viewing
them holistically and authentically — not merely
as consumers and shoppers completing a task,
but as people living full, rich lives.
