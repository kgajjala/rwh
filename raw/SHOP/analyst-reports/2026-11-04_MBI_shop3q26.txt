In 1Q'17, Shopify's Gross Merchandise Value (GMV) was ~10% of Amazon's estimated
retail GMV. It took them seven years to reach ~30%. After growing GMV 30% (FXN) in
3Q'26, their sixth consecutive quarter of 29-30% FXN growth, Shopify is now at ~46%
and looks likely to cross 50% around the middle of next year. If it does, going from
30% to 50% will have taken only about three years.

On an incremental basis, Shopify added ~$27 Billion of GMV YoY in 3Q’26 whereas
Amazon retail likely added ~$17 Billion, or ~158% of what Amazon added which is the
highest reading since I started tracking this, but we need to normalize this for Prime
Day change. Prime Day moved into 2Q this year for most of Amazon’s large countries
whereas it was entirely in 3Q last year, and Amazon’s CFO said the shift cost ~400 bps
of its overall 3Q growth. In hindsight, much of the moderation to ~80% that I noted in
the 2Q’26 update was this timing shift, and 158% is flattered by the same shift in
reverse. Putting the two quarters together is a better read.

During the call, Shopify’s CFO reminded the outsized incremental share gains of
Shopify. From the call (emphasis mine):
Indeed, GMV grew 33% FXN in Europe vs 28% in North America. It’s also not just e-
commerce, Shopify’s offline GMV also grew 30%. Moreover, B2B GMV grew 74%,
continuing a gradual deceleration from 84%, 80%, and 76% over the prior three
quarters as the base scales.
“Over the last three years, our U.S. merchants’ online sales grew at a compound
annual growth rate of 23%, compared to 8% for U.S. e-commerce overall. In Europe,
we saw a similar pattern. Our merchants grew online GMV at a compound annual
growth rate of 37%, compared to a market growth of 8%, doubling their share of e-
commerce in just three years. These trends have accelerated. Three years ago,
Shopify merchants drove nearly a fifth of incremental e-commerce sales in the U.S.
and nearly a quarter in Europe. Since the start of 2025, they now capture nearly
half in the U.S. and more than 40% in Europe.”

Gross margin fell ~130 bps YoY to 47.6% mostly due to the usual mix shift, as merchant
solutions (+35%) outgrew subscription solutions (+20%). Merchant solutions gross
margin actually expanded YoY for the third consecutive quarter, helped by savings
negotiated across the payments ecosystem, FX activity, and advertising. Subscription
gross margin, however, has now declined four quarters in a row.
Management was clear that incremental AI spend on Sidekick, its AI assistant for
merchants, was the primary reason for the YoY compression in subscription, partially
offset by efficiencies in support, but said it is not the sequential culprit. From 2Q to
3Q, merchant conversations grew 14%, cost per conversation fell 28%, and total
Sidekick cost fell 18% thanks to better model selection, distillation, and caching.
That is a pretty good data point on AI unit economics. The sequential decline was
attributed to hosting capacity added ahead of Black Friday and some storefront and
search work.

The question investors seem most focused on is how Shopify gets paid in the agentic
commerce world. It came up three times during the call. The first time, an analyst
asked how payment economics change depending on whether a shopper uses Shop
Pay, Meta’s wallet, or something else; the answer focused on agents being a “new front
door” and did not really address economics. The second time, the CFO gave a more
direct response (emphasis mine):
While Amazon objected to Muse not identifying itself as an agent, and on its 3Q call,
Andy Jassy said Alexa for Shopping is going to be the primary shopping agent
customers use on Amazon, Shopify’s merchants have no such agent of their own and
want the reach.
That ambition around Shop may also explain shop.com. for which Shopify paid almost
$100 million just for the domain (likely the highest ever for any domain ever). Here is
how Shopify defended it:
“If you start, for example, on Muse, and if you as a consumer then do a search which
leads to a merchant which is using Shopify, then the economics to us are exactly
the same. Essentially, this is another form of discovery. They’re sending you to the
merchant’s website, and everything else that you would have done in any other form
of discovery ends up to the same economics back to Shopify.
”
Look, in the physical retail world, location, location matters. We think the same thing
applies on the internet as well, and we think shop.com is an incredible prime
address for us. I think the way you should view this is this is a declaration that we
are ready for a more ambitious posture for Shop. We wanted to make a deliberate
investment to Shop’s credibility and discoverability as a consumer shopping
destination, not just a tracking utility.

As you can see above, only 6 of the 25 combinations clear 10% at a 30x exit, 3 at 25x,
and 12 at 35x. Each 2.5 points of revenue CAGR and each 2.5 points of margin are
worth largely the same ~2-3 points of annual return, and each 5 turns of exit multiple is
worth ~3-4 points. At today’s ~15% margin, none of these combinations gets to 10%;
even 25% growth and a 35x exit gets you only ~8%. So you need ~5-10 points margin
expansion,
~20-25% 5-year revenue growth CAGR, and 30x or higher exit multiple
simultaneously to get to low to mid-teen IRR. If you are confused why the stock is SO
volatile based on a point or two GMV growth beat/miss, this should be clarifying.
