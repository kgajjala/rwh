## xAI Merger (Feb 2, 2026) — pre-IPO, foundational to the AI segment
- SpaceX acquired xAI in an all-stock transaction; combined entity valued at **$1.25 trillion** at signing (SpaceX at $1T, xAI at $250B) — described by Reuters as the largest M&A deal ever at the time.
- Structure: share swap, xAI investors received 0.1433 SpaceX shares per xAI share.
- Strategic framing (Musk): positions the deal as enabling a long-term "data centers in space" strategy; SpaceX has requested FCC authorization to launch up to 1 million satellites for this "orbital data center" concept.
- **Colossus infrastructure** (originally built for internal xAI/Grok training): Colossus 2 went live January 2026 as the world's first gigawatt-scale AI training cluster, with a planned upgrade to 1.5GW by April 2026. Colossus (1) houses 220,000+ Nvidia GPUs across 300MW+ of power capacity (this is the facility later leased to Anthropic).
- Following the merger, SpaceX began transitioning Colossus capacity from internal-only (xAI/Grok) usage to leasing to external AI customers.

## Float / Short Interest / Lockup Structure (unusual technical setup)
- Total shares outstanding: ≈13 billion
- IPO floated only ≈555.6M new shares (≈639M including underwriter over-allotment) — **free float is only ≈4–5% of total shares outstanding**, versus a typical 10–25% for a new listing
- Short interest: ≈196M shares sold short = **≈31% of the free float** (not 31% of total shares) — an extreme short-as-%-of-float level enabled specifically by the unusually thin float

## Competitive Landscape Notes
- SpaceX launch market share: cited variously as ≈82% (global commercial launch), ≈87% (global orbital launch mass), >90% (broader estimate), ≈60% (commercial launches by mass, in a Rocket Lab-comparison context) — methodology varies by source; directionally SpaceX is the dominant, likely >80% player by most measures.
- **Blue Origin**: New Glenn flew first commercial missions early 2026; won a NASA lunar cargo contract (~$468M); **grounded since a launchpad explosion May 28, 2026**, widening SpaceX's near-term lead.
- **Rocket Lab**: Electron rocket, 21 launches in 2025 (2nd-most-frequent US launch vehicle); Q1 2026 backlog >$2.2B (+108% YoY) — smaller-scale/dedicated-smallsat niche, not a direct heavy-lift competitor.
- **Amazon Kuiper**: 3,236-satellite constellation fully licensed; FCC mandate requires 50% deployed by July 2026, "likely to be missed" per one source — regulatory risk for Kuiper. Notably, Kuiper must launch some satellites on competitors' rockets including SpaceX's own Falcon 9 (and ULA Vulcan, Blue Origin New Glenn).
- Satellite launch services market: grew from $10.34B (2024) to $11.9B (2025), projected to reach $22.18B by 2029 — this is the "traditional launch services" TAM, much smaller than SpaceX's own $28.5T claimed TAM (which leans almost entirely on AI).
