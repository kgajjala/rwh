SCOPE
- Approximately 1,000 Cybercab vehicles in Austin, TX.
- Opened the same day Tesla began charging fares for Cybercab rides (2026-09-03).

WHAT IS AT ISSUE
NHTSA will examine "the process and technical data on which Tesla relied when
certifying" the Cybercab, and specifically "the extent to which Tesla's
certification depended on determinations that certain FMVSS are inapplicable
to the Cybercab."

MECHANISM
- The US uses manufacturer SELF-certification; there is no pre-market approval.
- Many Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards were written assuming a human
  driver with steering wheel, brake pedal and mirrors.
- The Cybercab has no steering wheel, no pedals and no mirrors. Tesla determined
  certain FMVSS simply do not apply to it, and certified on that basis.
- The audit does NOT evaluate self-driving performance. It evaluates whether the
  certification process itself was proper.

TESLA POSITION
Tesla told regulators it "certified the vehicles as compliant with all applicable
Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards" and plans gradual deployment expansion.

PRECEDENT
Zoox was subject to a comparable certification question that set its program back
roughly four years.

CONTEXT — OTHER OPEN NHTSA MATTERS
- Existing probe into FSD covering ~3.2 million vehicles (visibility-related crashes).
- Documented incident in which a remote Tesla operator crashed a Robotaxi in Houston.
