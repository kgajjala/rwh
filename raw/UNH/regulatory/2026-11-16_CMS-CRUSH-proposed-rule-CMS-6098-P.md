Medicare Advantage provisions (fact sheet, via WebFetch summary):
- RADV: CMS proposes to codify that it will NOT apply a fee-for-service adjuster, set the sampling methodology, and begin extrapolating audit findings with payment year (PY) 2018 audits. CMS estimates ~$9 billion per payment year in MA overpayments collected, on average.
- Formal RADV hardship-exception framework for medical-record submission.
- Proposed §422.222(a): all eligible providers/suppliers billing MA must be enrolled in Original Medicare in approved status; effective plan year beginning two years after the final rule. PACE excepted.
- Mandatory MA/Part D fraud referrals (CY2025: only 46% of Part C/D contracts reported referrals voluntarily; 19% reported payment suspensions).
- Whole rule: "nearly $90 billion in federal budget savings and about $378.51 million in costs from 2027 to 2036" (Medicare, Medicaid/CHIP, Marketplace). No program split in the fact sheet.
- Comments: 60 days after Federal Register publication (Jan 19, 2027 per secondary sources).
- Press release quotes: RFK Jr.: "This proposal could save taxpayers nearly $90 billion over 10 years". Oz: "Every taxpayer dollar lost to fraud is a dollar that cannot support the care these programs were created to provide."

Litigation background:
- Humana v. Becerra (N.D. Tex.), Sep 25, 2025: 2023 RADV final rule vacated on procedural (logical-outgrowth / notice) grounds; court did not reach the lawfulness of extrapolation or dropping the FFS adjuster. https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/court-vacates-cmss-2023-final-rule-on-radv-audits
- CMS appealed Nov 21, 2025 (Fifth Circuit No. 25-11293, Humana v. Kennedy). https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/cms-appeals-humana-v-becerra
- Oral argument Oct 5, 2026; STAT: panel "seemed poised to toss the rule out, again." Decision pending. https://www.statnews.com/2026/10/05/humana-medicare-advantage-radv-audits-lawsuit-hearing/
- CRUSH re-proposes the same policy through notice and comment, which addresses the procedural defect but not the substantive (actuarial equivalence, retroactivity) arguments the district court left open. [Author's reading]
