## Verdict

**Adobe keeps beating its own numbers, but the growth underneath is slowing: organic ARR is up ~9%, price increases are on hold, and the board has handed the company to the head of its smaller business. At $239 the probability-weighted case pays 9.9% a year, and almost all of it comes from buybacks rather than growth.**

**Breaks if**: the FY27 ARR guide in December (the new CEO's first) implies organic growth below ~8%. The buyback can carry EPS for a while, but not for a book of business slowing toward GDP growth.

## The Call

The market has stopped paying for Adobe's growth. At **≈9.8× non-GAAP and 13× GAAP earnings**, spot prices roughly zero growth forever on SBC-adjusted free cash flow (§5). Meanwhile the business still grew revenue 13% in Q3, took AI-first ARR past $650M (+150%) and reached 1 billion monthly users.

The market has a point, though. Strip out the SEMrush acquisition and **total ARR grew ~9%**, Q3 net new ARR was $0.40B, and the Creative Cloud price increases that carried 2024–25 were deferred in June so that the freemium push could run. That push now has **100M+ free creative users** and no disclosed conversion rate.

Then the succession. The board picked **Anil Chakravarthy**, who built the customer-experience business. The executive running the creative business, which generates roughly three-quarters of revenue, is leaving, and the CFO chair is still filled on an interim basis. The stock fell 7% on the announcement and is down 17% since Sep 1.

What keeps the case alive is the buyback: **~9.5M shares a quarter, share count −6.8% in a year**. That alone turns 6% revenue growth into ~10% EPS growth (§5). It is a good return, but not 13%. **The entry that clears the hurdle is $207**, a price the stock traded below as recently as July.

## What I'd Have To Be Wrong About

1. **That freemium converts.** Test: the FY27 ARR guide (~Dec 10) and any paid-conversion disclosure at MAX (Nov) or Q1 FY27 (Mar 2027). A guide below 9% total, with no conversion metric, means the free funnel is replacing paid seats rather than feeding them.
2. **That the creative franchise survives a leadership change centred on the customer-experience business.** Test: Creative & Marketing Professionals subscription growth in Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 (Dec, Mar). Below 10% (it was 13% in Q3), or further senior creative departures, means the transition is costing the core.
3. **That AI costs don't take the margin.** Test: the FY27 non-GAAP operating-margin guide. Q4 is already guided to ~44%, against 46% for FY25. A guide below 42% would make EPS growth depend entirely on the buyback.

Debt of $6.4B is almost fully covered by $5.6B of cash, with $1.6B due within twelve months.

The **Base** is attach plus buyback on ~6% revenue growth. The **Bull** needs freemium conversion and the deferred price increases to land together in FY27–28. Both are options, valued inside the Bull rather than as separate lines (§5). ## 3. Moat, Customer & Competition

| Read | Call | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | **Switching costs** + **scale economies** + branding | PSD/PDF/AI/INDD formats and decades of trained skills; 1B MAU; Firefly's licensed-content training is the enterprise-safe claim |
| Trend | **Narrowing** | Organic ARR growth ~9% vs. 11–13% in FY23–24; price increases deferred to defend the funnel; AI generation erodes the skill premium Photoshop monetized |

## 4. Management & Capital

⚠️ **Leadership is the window's main event.** On Sep 2 the board named **Anil Chakravarthy** (head of Customer Experience Orchestration and worldwide field operations since 2020) CEO effective **Dec 1**; **Shantanu Narayen** becomes Executive Chair ([8-K](../../../raw/ADBE/filings/2026-09-08_8-K-CEO-appointment.txt)). The same filing disclosed that **David Wadhwani**, President of Creativity & Productivity, would step down on Sep 27. **Steve Day** remains interim CFO since Jun 15 (Form 4, Sep 15), and the Chief Legal Officer was given a retention letter in July that runs until twelve months after the new CEO starts ([8-K](../../../raw/ADBE/filings/2026-07-17_8-K-CLO-retention.txt)). That is a board protecting continuity.

**Incentives**: the annual cash plan pays on **revenue and non-GAAP EPS** (95% threshold). Performance shares are **50% relative TSR vs. the Nasdaq-100** (55th-percentile target) and **50% "net new sales"**: Digital Media ARR growth plus customer-experience subscription growth ([2026 proxy](../../../raw/ADBE/filings/2026-02-27_DEF14A.txt)). The 2023 grant's TSR leg paid 45%. Two notes: non-GAAP EPS excludes the ~8% SBC, and the freemium pivot **deliberately depresses the ARR metric that pays half the PSP**. A management cutting its own incentive metric is evidence the bet is sincere. **Guidance grade: Consistent beater** on revenue and EPS; the one blemish is June's ~$500M organic ARR cut. Deciding number: Q2 revenue $6.62B against a $6.43–6.48B guide.

💰 **Outsider grade: Outsider** (held). FY25 retired $11.3B of stock, and FY26 continues at ~$2.2B a quarter around $235, roughly 9–10× non-GAAP earnings, with no dividend and one modest acquisition. The caveat: SBC reissues roughly a quarter of what is bought, so the net share count falls ~7% a year, not the ~9% that gross spend implies.

**Appearances**: the [Q3 call](../../../raw/ADBE/appearances/2026-09-10_Q3-FY26-earnings-call_narayen-day.txt) (Sep 10) and the CEO announcement (Sep 3). Day framed the strategy as *"expanding our user base through a freemium strategy"*; Chakravarthy's only stated theme is *"agentic software"* across creativity, productivity and customer experience. A full verbatim Q3 transcript could not be recovered, so conversion and pricing commentary is unconfirmed. No conference or media appearance by Narayen or Chakravarthy was found in the window. Narayen sold **125,000 shares (~$31M) at $246–254** on Sep 16–17, a round lot consistent with a planned sale.
