## Business Overview

Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN) runs three durable Wide-moat franchises stitched together by a customer-obsession operating culture: **the world's largest e-commerce retail and marketplace** business, **AWS** — the world's largest cloud-infrastructure platform, now at a **$169B annualized run rate** — and **advertising**, at a ~$77B run rate and the third-largest digital ad business behind Google and Meta. Optionality stacks on top: Kuiper LEO satellites, Zoox robotaxis, Amazon Pharmacy and One Medical, MGM Studios, and equity positions in both Anthropic and OpenAI.

The founding insight (Bezos, 1994) was that the internet would commoditize selection and price discovery, and that a company obsessed with customer experience while ploughing every dollar of profit back into capability would compound at rates traditional retailers could not. The 2002–2006 AWS pivot extended the same logic — Amazon already ran internet-scale infrastructure for itself, and productizing it created the most profitable cloud business in history. **2026 is the third generational bet**: AI infrastructure at ~$220B of annual capex, aimed at owning where the next decade of compute demand lives.

**FY2025 results** ([Q4 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000002/amzn-20251231xex991.htm)): revenue **$716.9B** (+12%), operating income **$80.0B** (11.2% margin), net income $77.7B, diluted EPS $7.17, AWS operating income $45.6B, and free cash flow already compressed to **$7.7B** from $32.9B in FY2024. FY2026 is tracking to roughly $790–800B of revenue — and to negative free cash flow.

Jassy's framing has been unusually direct. The *FY25 letter*: *"We're not going to be conservative in how we play this — we're investing to be the meaningful leader."*

## Pivotal Investment Question

> **AWS just accelerated to +37% and booked a $496B backlog while Amazon simultaneously became a major shareholder in two of its largest AI customers. How much of this demand exists independently of Amazon's own capital?**

The bull answer is: nearly all of it. The backlog is contracted across thousands of enterprise customers, AWS operating margin expanded to 39.4%, and the OpenAI and Anthropic relationships are commercial arrangements that any hyperscaler would have competed hard to win. The bear answer is that Amazon has committed **$50B to OpenAI** and holds an Anthropic stake it just marked up by **~$50.5B**, and that both entities are AWS customers — so a meaningful slice of the reported revenue growth *and* the reported profit traces back to Amazon's own balance sheet.

Both can be true at once, and the honest position is that this is not yet resolvable from outside. What is resolvable is the accounting question underneath it: at $264 the stock trades at **21× reported** trailing earnings and **~34× on operating earnings**, and generates **negative** free cash flow. The market is pricing the first number.

### Q2 FY2026 — the quarter's two halves

**The operating half was excellent.** Revenue crossed $200B for the first time. AWS accelerated to **+37%**, its fastest in 18 quarters, against Street expectations near +31%, taking the annualized run rate to $169B. AWS operating income rose **+64%** to $16.6B on a **39.4% margin**, and the disclosed **backlog reached $496B** of contracted, not-yet-recognized work. Advertising added 26% to reach $19.8B. All three segments were profitable: AWS $16.6B, North America $9.1B (7.9% margin), International $1.7B (4.1%). Consolidated operating income rose 43% to $27.5B. Amazon also disclosed that its AI business and its chips business each exceed a **$25B annual run rate**, both growing triple digits.

**The reported half needs unwinding.** Net income of **$62.6B** — more than triple the prior year — includes a **~$50.5B upward revaluation of Amazon's Anthropic non-voting preferred stock** (~$62.8B across the six-month period). That single non-cash, non-operating item is roughly **twice the entire quarter's operating profit**, and it is explicitly *removed* in the operating-cash-flow reconciliation. Reported diluted EPS of $5.75 is therefore not a measure of what the business earned. Applying a ~20% tax assumption to the six-month marks removes roughly **$4.61/share** from trailing EPS, taking TTM EPS from $12.43 to **≈$7.82** *[Estimate]* — which is the number the ~34× multiple in §8 is built on.

**And the cash half turned negative.** Q2 capex of **$53.1B** drove quarterly free cash flow to **–$7.7B**, with trailing-twelve-month FCF at **–$7.6B** on ~$169B of TTM capex. Management raised the 2026 cash capex guide from ~$200B to **~$220B**, attributing part of the increase to **higher memory prices** — an input-cost increase rather than incremental capacity. Q3 revenue guidance of **$197–202B** landed below the ~$204.1B consensus.

### Primary Source: 10-K Segment Detail (FY2024 + multi-year MD&A)

The 5-year segment evolution (FY21 → FY25) tells a clean story that aggregator headlines flatten:

- **AWS op income trajectory**: $18.5B → $22.8B → $24.6B → $39.8B → $45.6B. The **FY24 step-change (+62% YoY)** was the "cost optimization attenuating" inflection Jassy flagged in the *FY23 letter*: *"By the end of 2023, we saw cost optimization attenuating, new deals accelerating, customers renewing at larger commitments over longer time periods."* AWS op margin expanded from ≈24% (FY22) to ≈37% (FY24) on operating leverage as the customer-optimization cycle ended.
- **North America: structural turn**. NA op income went **–$2.8B (FY22) → +$29.6B (FY25)** — a **+$32B swing in 3 years**. Drivers from MD&A: regionalization of fulfillment (sub-day delivery without cost penalty), advertising operating leverage (≈50% margins on $60–70B run rate), and per-unit cost-to-serve declining for the first time since 2018 ([FY23 letter](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-ceo-andy-jassy-2023-letter-to-shareholders): *"In 2023, for the first time since 2018, we reduced our cost to serve on a per unit basis globally"*).
- **International: profitability inflection**. Intl op income went **–$7.7B (FY22) → +$4.7B (FY25)** — first time profitable at scale; same regionalization playbook applied to UK / EU / India / Brazil.
- **Capex acceleration**: $52.7B (FY23) → $83B (FY24) → ≈$100B (FY25) → **$220B (FY26 guide)** — a 4× acceleration over 3 years, and the reason free cash flow has gone negative. The FY25 letter's framing: *"We only start monetizing this capital investment many months after we spend the capital, and over many years — which leads to attractive long-term FCF and ROIC"* ([FY24 letter](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-ceo-andy-jassy-2024-letter-to-shareholders)).

The MD&A read changes the thesis from "Amazon is spending more on AI" to "Amazon's three-segment op income engine just printed +$45B in 3 years and is being entirely re-deployed into AI infrastructure with disclosed multi-year customer commitments."

AWS share trend versus Azure remains the cleanest real-time indicator; **Q3's AWS growth rate against the +37% comp is the single most important number on October 29.**

**[Outsider grade](../../frameworks/outsiders.md) — Reinvestor, with a new caveat**: Bezos's FCF-per-share and decentralized "two-pizza" doctrine is Outsider canon, and minimal buybacks are *consistent* with the rule when reinvestment IRR genuinely exceeds the alternative. But the capital being deployed in 2026 is a different kind. The **$50B OpenAI investment — completed with a final $35B tranche in July 2026 — is capital placed into a customer that then committed to $100B+ of AWS purchases over eight years.** That is closer to vendor financing than to the 2007–2015 AWS build, and it makes reported returns harder to audit from outside: Amazon books the AWS revenue, holds the equity, and marks the equity to market. None of this is improper, and the demand is contracted — but "reinvestment IRR exceeds the alternative" is a claim that becomes much harder to verify when the company is on both sides of the transaction. Grade held at Reinvestor; the countercyclical-repurchase test remains untested and is now further away, since negative FCF removes the capacity for one.

## Section 13 — Recommendation & Bottom Line

**Thesis in one sentence**: Amazon's operating business is performing better than at any point in this page's history — AWS +37% with a $496B contracted backlog, operating income +43%, all three segments profitable — but the reported $62.6B of quarterly net income was carried by a ~$50.5B non-cash mark on an Anthropic stake, free cash flow has turned negative against ~$220B of 2026 capex, and Amazon now holds equity in two of the AI customers driving its growth, which makes the 21× headline multiple (~34× on operating earnings) a worse guide to what you are paying than at any time in the last decade.

**For a non-holder**: 🟡 **Watch** — the operating case is strong and spot sits just inside a defensible entry, but a ~7%/yr expected return with negative FCF, zero Sell ratings across 60 analysts, and no catalyst before October 29 does not argue for urgency. Prefer initiating below **$250**, or after Q3 confirms FCF is troughing.

**For a current holder**: 🟡 **Hold** — nothing here argues for reducing a position in a business compounding operating income at 43%. Equally, nothing argues for adding at a 75th-percentile price into a quarter that has to answer three open questions at once.

🎯 **Attractive entry zone**: **$230 – $277** — a 25% margin of safety to the $370 PW EV; spot sits near the top of this band, which is why the verb is Watch rather than Initiate.

🎯 **Trim zone**: **$370 – $550** — at or above PW EV, where the Base case is priced and the Bull case is not yet proved.

🔴 **Exit / avoid zone**: **>$550** — Bull-case territory; requires FY2031 operating EPS ≈$22 at a 25× multiple simultaneously.

**Thesis-break triggers** ⚠️ (would force re-rating toward Reduce / Exit):

- ⚠️ **A material *downward* revaluation of the Anthropic or OpenAI stakes** — would confirm that the earnings quality concern was the right frame, and would likely coincide with slowing AWS growth rather than offsetting it
- ⚠️ **Free cash flow remains negative through FY2027** — would mean the capex cycle has no visible trough and the capital-light platform framing no longer applies
- ⚠️ AWS YoY revenue growth decelerates below +20% for two consecutive quarters (raised from +18% given the new +37% base)
- ⚠️ FY2027 capex guided above $250B without commensurate AWS acceleration — supply-driven over-investment
- ⚠️ FY2027 capex guided *down* more than 10% — the demand-weakness read of the same signal
- ⚠️ AWS operating margin falls below 32% — the overcapacity/price-competition signature in the Bear case
- ⚠️ Advertising growth decelerates below +15%
- ⚠️ FTC structural remedy ordered at the February 2027 trial
- ⚠️ Any officer-level discretionary selling *outside* the existing 10b5-1 plans

📅 **Next review trigger**: **Q3 FY2026 earnings — October 29, 2026**. In priority order: (1) **free cash flow — is it troughing or still deteriorating**, (2) **AWS growth against the +37% comp**, (3) the size and direction of any new Anthropic/OpenAI revaluation, (4) whether the ~$220B FY2026 capex guide is reaffirmed, raised, or cut, (5) AWS operating margin against 39.4%, (6) backlog progression from $496B, (7) advertising growth against +26%.
