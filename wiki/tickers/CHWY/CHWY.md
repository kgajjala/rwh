**The dominant US online pet specialist, with 85% of sales on subscription, priced as if its margins have stopped expanding: at $18.27 the probability-weighted case pays 13.7% a year, just over the hurdle, so it is an Initiate with almost no cushion and a sponsor still owning 44%.**

💰 **Outsider grade: Outsider-leaning**, surfaced on a cluster of capital-allocation events (§4): a doubled buyback authorization, $400M repurchased in H1 at a $22.88 average, a first-ever $600M term loan, and $575M of health M&A.

**Breaks if**: the margin path stalls. A FY2027 plan that holds the adjusted EBITDA margin near 6.8% to buy growth removes most of the Base case's per-share compounding.

## The Call

Chewy has lost **55% since last October** on three things the market is extrapolating: a sales-guide cut eleven weeks after the CEO said on CNBC he was *"nothing but bullish"* on 2026, a dog-and-premium slowdown that hit treats and toppers, and a Q2 beat padded by ~$15M of tariff refunds and breakage. The result is an EV of ≈$7.3B, **8× guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA**, for a business still adding 170–250K customers a quarter.

What the price misses is the recurring core. **Autoship sales grew 9.3% in Q2** against 7.3% for total sales — the subscription base is compounding at roughly twice the pet market's ~4%, and gross margin held near 30% in what the CFO called *"a very low price environment."* Adjusted EBITDA margin has risen ~100 bps a year for two years without price; SBC is flat in dollars, so each point of margin reaches per-share earnings with leverage. The company bought 9.9M of its own shares in Q2 at **$20.22**.

The price is honest about one thing: after stock comp, TTM free cash flow is only **$270M, a 3.7% yield**. The thesis is that margin, not multiple, closes that gap. The return clears the hurdle by 70 bps, and a controlling sponsor with a fresh shelf can supply stock at any time — the entry is cheap, but not cheaply earned.

## What I'd Have To Be Wrong About

1. **That the margin can keep expanding without starving growth.** Test: the FY2027 guide, ~late March 2027. Adjusted EBITDA margin guided ≥7.3% with organic sales ≥5% keeps the Base path; a flat margin framed as "reinvestment" (the CFO already called 2027 spend *"part of the 2027 planning process"*) cuts the Base.
2. **That the customer base is still growing.** Test: the Dec 9 and March prints. Net adds of ≥150K a quarter — the low end of management's own range — or better. A net decline, as in FY2022–23, means share gain has stalled and the Bear odds rise.
3. **That buybacks outrun stock-comp issuance.** Test: the Q4 FY2026 10-K. FY2026 FCF ≥$600M with SBC ~$300M and year-end diluted shares ≤410M. Diluted shares rising while the company borrows means the buyback is decorative.

## 3. Moat, Customer & Competition

| Read | Call | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | **Switching costs** (habit) + **scale economies** | Autoship subscriptions, stored pet and prescription profiles; 17 fulfillment centres reaching 80% of the US overnight; retail-media data brands pay for |
| Trend | **Stable** | Autoship share up 14 pts in four years and customers growing again; against that, no pricing power this cycle and a membership program that missed its own forecast |
| Profit pool | Brands capture most; shifting slowly to Chewy | Mars, Nestlé Purina, Hill's own the consumables margin; sponsored ads, private label and vet services move some to Chewy |
| Customer | 21.7M pet households, bought for *never running out* of food and medication | Autoship 84.6% of sales; ~40% of clinic customers new to Chewy; last price increase n/a — pricing flat this cycle; CAC and LTV n/d |

**Narrow, stable**: habit is real but not contractual, and the category's largest sellers — Amazon and Walmart — can match price whenever they choose.

| Competitor | Position | Threat |
|---|---|---|
| **Amazon** | Largest general online seller of pet consumables; share n/d | The one that can price Chewy's core below cost. Singh declined to discuss its same-day push (Q1 call). |
| **Walmart** | Stores, online, pet pharmacy | Price leader for value shoppers; the trade-down risk if the consumer weakens further. |
| **Petco** | ~$6B of sales; stores with vet hospitals | Losing share (comps +0.6%) but stabilizing; the clinic model Chewy is now copying. |
| **Veterinarians** | ~$40B services market | Control prescriptions; the reason Chewy is buying clinics. |

**Industry**: store-based pet retail is flat and online gains share. Chewy is strongest where the purchase recurs (food, medication) and weakest where it is discretionary (treats, toys).

## 4. Management & Capital

**Sumit Singh** (CEO since 2018, ex-Amazon) has run the company since before the IPO. Finance has turned over: **Chris Deppe** (CFO since February 2026, ex-Amazon, internal) is the third CFO since 2023, and the CTO retired in February. Governance is **controlled**: BC Partners holds all 176.5M Class B shares, **44% of the economics and 88% of the votes**; a five-year Class B sunset agreed in the 2023 PetSmart-tax downstream merger ends that control around late 2028 `[Derived]`.

**Incentives**: annual bonus 50% net-sales growth / 50% adjusted EBITDA margin; performance RSUs 50% net sales / 30% adjusted EBITDA margin / 20% free cash flow on a one-year period, then cliff-vesting ([2026 proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1766502/000114036126022529/ny20064174x1_def14a.htm)). No per-share or return-on-capital metric, and adjusted EBITDA excludes stock comp — the plan pays for growth, not for dilution control. Singh's FY2025 pay was $25.7M. **Guidance grade: Mixed** — the FY2026 margin guide has held or risen at every update, but the sales guide fell **$200M at the midpoint** eleven weeks after it was set.
