**Breaks if**: the DICK'S segment comp guide (+2.5% to +4.0%) is cut. It survived Q2 untouched; that single fact carries the whole thesis.

## The Call

On August 25 DKS fell **30.7% in one session — the worst day in its history** — on a FY2026 guidance cut. Read the cut by segment and it is not a company-wide failure: of the ~$260M reduction in operating income, **~$190M is Foot Locker** (segment profit +$110–150M → a $40–80M *loss*) and only ~$70M is DICK'S margin. The DICK'S comp guide did not move at all, on the back of a quarter that comped **+4.9%** with gross margin **+79 bps** and ~200 bps of share gain against a declining industry.

Against $1.54–1.60B of guided DICK'S segment profit, the enterprise value still pays a single-digit EBIT multiple for the good business and assigns Foot Locker a **negative** one (§4) — with GameChanger and the Media Network free. That is the mispricing.

It is not free money. Foot Locker's true cost was never the $2.5B headline but **~$6B** once ~$2.8B of assumed leases and up to $750M of restructuring are counted, and FY2025 free cash flow of $400M already came in *below* the $414M dividend. The wager is that a fixable segment is being priced as a permanent one.

## What I'd Have To Be Wrong About

1. **That Foot Locker is cyclical, not structurally dead.** Test: Q4 FY2026, when its comps first enter the reported base instead of being shown proforma. A print below the –2.0% floor with the loss past $(80)M means the fleet is shrinking faster than the turnaround can fix it.
2. **That the DICK'S business is insulated.** Management pre-flagged Q3 gross margin as *"most pronounced."* Margin pressure I accept; a cut to the **comp** guide on Nov 24 would mean the promotional cycle reached demand, not just price — and the sum-of-the-parts collapses.
3. **That the balance sheet funds the wait.** Test: the Q3 10-Q cash-flow statement. If FY2026 FCF lands below the ~$450M dividend *and* FY2027 capex does not step down from $1.6B, this stops being a valuation story and becomes a funding one.

## 1. Business & Numbers

Two segments, different economics. The **DICK'S Business** (892 stores, 46.0M sq ft — DICK'S, House of Sport, Field House, Golf Galaxy, Going Going Gone!, Public Lands, plus GameChanger) did record FY2025 sales of $14.11B on +4.5% comps. The **Foot Locker Business** (2,478 doors — Foot Locker, Kids Foot Locker, Champs Sports, WSS, atmos) was bought in September 2025 for $2.5B, 84% in stock at ≈$219, and is being cut hard: 110 doors closed in H1, WSS from 143 to 99.

## 2. Moat & Competition

**Narrow.** Three sources, most durable first: **allocation privilege** — the largest US wholesale door gets depth and exclusivity subscale rivals cannot (the flip side of the Nike concentration in §5); **ScoreCard + GameChanger data**, ~30M members driving >75% of sales, acquiring families at the start of the equipment cycle; and **experiential real estate**, where House of Sport buys landlord leverage and brand-partner priority. Against that: no switching costs, and no pricing power in a promotional cycle — Q2 proved it, as DICK'S *chose* to cut price to defend share.

| Competitor | Position | Threat |
|---|---|---|
| **Nike (DTC)** | ~35% US brand share; DKS's largest vendor | The dependency and the threat at once. Its DTC pivot disintermediates wholesale; the current retreat is a reprieve, not a settlement. At a 12-year low ([NKE](../NKE/NKE.md)), so the brand cycle driving DKS's promotions is unfixed. |
| **JD Sports (+Hibbett)** | Global sneaker retail | Direct Foot Locker rival with the same mall exposure — and *also* closing ~175 Hibbett doors. Capacity is leaving the channel. |
| **Academy Sports** | ~$6B value-oriented | Real share competitor in DICK'S core, positioned to absorb closing WSS and Hibbett doors. Lower price point, weaker brand access. |
| **Amazon** | Structural | Takes the undifferentiated equipment tail; weakest against launch product, fitting and experiential formats. |

**Industry**: a ~$120.6B US market ([IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/market-size/sporting-goods-stores/1079/)) whose footwear cycle is mid-correction — inventory clearing through promotion, demand rotating from legacy retro silhouettes toward newer performance product, precisely Foot Locker's over-exposure. The whole complex is drawn down together ([NKE](../NKE/NKE.md) 12-year low, [ONON](../ONON/ONON.md) –43%, [LULU](../LULU/LULU.md) near multi-year lows), which argues category rather than DKS execution — and equally means nothing ends it soon. Capacity leaving the channel is the precondition for FY2028+ recovery, and scale survivors capture it.

## 3. Management & Capital

**Lauren Hobart** (CEO since 2021) runs the company; **Ed Stack** (Executive Chairman, running it since 1984) still oversees merchandising and personally leads the Foot Locker integration — the 10-K names both as key-person risks. Governance is **dual-class**: Stack, relatives and family trusts control a majority of combined voting power.

💰 **Outsider grade: Steward (not Outsider).** Share count fell ~24% FY2021→FY2024 and the dividend has grown 12 straight years — but the timing is anti-Singleton. The biggest buyback year was FY2021 ($1,145M) at COVID-peak prices alongside a $5.50 special dividend, and H1 FY2026 spent just **$141M at a $196.38 average** weeks before the stock hit $124, leaving **$3.0B authorized and unused**. Cutting the other way: funding the acquisition in stock at ≈$219 used expensive currency well. **Deploying that authorization here is the cleanest test this company has faced.**

**Primary sources.** Pattern C — an annual CEO letter inside the annual report. The [FY2025 letter](../../../raw/DKS/shareholder-letters/2025_letter.txt) is the most consequential document here, because it made a dated, falsifiable promise Q2 broke: *"Back to school is also when we expect an inflection point in both sales and profitability for the Foot Locker Business."* It produced a –3.6% comp and a $31.9M loss instead. The same letter's store-rollout target was delivered and exceeded — **the initiative shipped on schedule; the market it shipped into deteriorated.**

**Appearances (last 90 days)** — one: the [Q2 call](../../../raw/DKS/appearances/2026-08-25_Q2-FY26-earnings-call_stack-hobart-gupta.txt) (Aug 25). No conference, interview or podcast in the window. Off-script beyond the release: Fast Break to 300–350 doors; ScoreCard+ at $99/yr; promotion *"challenging at least through the fourth quarter"*; Q3 margin *"most pronounced."* Stack on spending into it: *"I don't think there's a better time to do this right now when the market is in a bit of turmoil."* ⚠️ **No buyback commentary and no profitability date.**
