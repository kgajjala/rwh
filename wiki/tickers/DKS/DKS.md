## Business Overview

DICK'S is the largest US specialty sporting-goods retailer and, since September 2025, the owner of Foot Locker — two segments with genuinely different economics. The **DICK'S Business** (892 stores, 46.0M sq ft: DICK'S, House of Sport, Field House, Golf Galaxy, Going Going Gone!, Public Lands, plus GameChanger) delivered record FY2025 sales of $14.11B on +4.5% comps, compounding through format repositioning rather than door count. The **Foot Locker Business** (2,478 locations across Foot Locker, Kids Foot Locker, Champs Sports, WSS and atmos in North America, Europe and Asia-Pacific) is a mall- and high-street-anchored sneaker retailer bought for $2.5B — 84% in stock, 9.6M shares at ≈$219 — now being restructured hard.

Three assets separate DICK'S from a commodity box retailer: **ScoreCard** (~30M active members, >75% of sales); **GameChanger**, a youth-sports streaming and scorekeeping SaaS platform; and **House of Sport**, the experiential format that earns landlord leverage and brand-partner priority. Roughly three-quarters of DICK'S leases come up for renewal at the company's option within five years — unusual optionality for a fleet this size.

## 3. Competitive Moat & Landscape

### Moat Rating: **Narrow**

Sources of advantage, in order of durability:
- **Allocation privilege.** As the largest US wholesale door, DICK'S receives depth and exclusivity from key brands that subscale competitors cannot obtain — the flip side of the Nike concentration risk. Premium full-service footwear decks now sit in ~90% of DICK'S stores.
- **ScoreCard + GameChanger data.** ~30M members driving >75% of sales, and a youth-sports platform with ~10M active users that acquires families at the earliest point of the equipment purchase cycle. GameChanger is the one genuinely non-retail asset in the portfolio.
- **Experiential real estate.** House of Sport gives DICK'S landlord negotiating power and access to A-locations; the format is expensive to copy and its economics are why capex has quadrupled.

Vulnerabilities: no switching costs, no pricing power in a promotional cycle — Q2 proved it, as DICK'S *chose* to cut price to defend share — and fashion-cycle exposure it does not control.

### Competitive Landscape

| Competitor | Position | Threat read |
|---|---|---|
| **Nike (DTC)** | ~35% US athletic brand share; DKS's largest vendor at ~31% of purchases | The structural threat and the structural dependency at once. Nike's DTC pivot disintermediates wholesale; its current retreat from that pivot is a temporary reprieve, not a settlement. Nike's own 12-year-low price ([NKE](../NKE/NKE.md)) means the brand cycle driving DKS's promotional pressure is not yet fixed. |
| **JD Sports (incl. Hibbett)** | Global sneaker retailer; acquired Hibbett for $1.11B (2024) | Direct Foot Locker competitor with the same mall exposure — and it is *also* rationalizing, closing ~175 Hibbett doors. Capacity is leaving the channel, which helps whoever survives. |
| **Academy Sports (ASO)** | ~$6B US value-oriented sporting goods | Genuine share competitor in DICK'S core, positioned to pick up doors as WSS and Hibbett shrink. Lower price point, less brand access. |
| **Amazon / marketplaces** | Structural | Takes the undifferentiated equipment tail; weakest against launch product, fitting, and experiential formats. |

How DICK'S differs: it is the only US player combining national scale, top-tier brand allocation, and an experiential format brands will build into — evidenced by Q2's share gain against a declining industry. The honest tail risk is that this is a *category* down-cycle in which even the strongest operator loses margin, which is exactly what the DICK'S segment-profit guide cut showed.

## 4. Management & Leadership

**Lauren Hobart** (President & CEO since 2021) runs the operating company; **Ed Stack** (Executive Chairman, running the business since 1984) still oversees merchandising and personally leads the Foot Locker integration — the 10-K names both as key-person risks. Governance is **dual-class**: Stack, his relatives and family trusts hold Class B shares controlling a majority of combined voting power.

### Capital Allocation Track Record

Share count fell ~24% from 109.6M (FY2021) to 82.9M (FY2024) before the Foot Locker issuance took it to ~90M; dividends have grown 12 consecutive years, with ~$2.4B returned over three years.

**Outsider grade: Steward (not Outsider)** — the buyback record is pro-cyclical, the direct inverse of Thorndike's central test. The largest repurchase year was FY2021 ($1,145M) at COVID-peak prices alongside a $5.50 special dividend; H1 FY2026 spent only **$141M at a $196.38 average** weeks before the stock traded at $124, leaving **$3.0B unused** and unmentioned on the Q2 call. One act cuts the other way — funding 84% of the acquisition in stock at ≈$219 was an Outsider-consistent use of expensive currency. **Deploying that authorization here is the cleanest Outsider test available; declining would confirm the grade.**

### Recent Management Commentary — Primary Source Synthesis

DKS is a **Pattern C** publisher — an annual CEO letter inside the annual report. The FY2025 letter ([2025 Annual Report](../../../raw/DKS/shareholder-letters/2025_letter.txt), May 2026) is the most consequential document on this page, because it made a dated, falsifiable commitment Q2 broke: *"Back to school is also when we expect an inflection point in both sales and profitability for the Foot Locker Business."* It produced a –3.6% comp and a $31.9M loss instead. That split is the investable point: **the initiative shipped on schedule; the market it shipped into deteriorated.** Stack's framing supports it — *"The inventory built up across the industry supply chains and across the retail marketplace, which led to an increasingly aggressive promotional environment"* — as does his countercyclical instinct: *"I don't think there's a better time to do this right now when the market is in a bit of turmoil."* But credibility has been spent, and the inflection date was replaced with nothing firmer than *"still early in the Foot Locker turnaround."*

## 5. Strategic Growth Initiatives

- **House of Sport / Field House** — 41 and 52 locations at Aug 1 (from 35 and 42 at year-end); ~14 and ~20 more planned this year, with ~18 House of Sport under construction for 2027. The square-footage engine, and the reason capex is stepped up.

- **GameChanger** — ~$150M FY2025 revenue, ~40% CAGR since 2017, ~10M active users; a Feb 2026 upgrade added HD streaming and coaching tools. High-margin subscription revenue inside a retailer.
- **DICK'S Media Network** — ~20B impressions in FY2025; retail-media margin that, with GameChanger, drove Q2's +79 bps of DICK'S gross-margin expansion.
- **ScoreCard+** — new $99/year paid tier, an untested attempt to convert a 30M-member free program into recurring revenue.
- **Foot Locker synergies** — $100–125M reaffirmed, primarily procurement and direct sourcing, over the medium term.

## 7. Industry-Specific Macro Analysis

The US sporting-goods store market is ~$120.6B (2026E, [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/market-size/sporting-goods-stores/1079/)), and DKS is the clear #1 specialty operator. Two structural dynamics govern the thesis.

**The footwear cycle is mid-correction, not post-correction.** Industry inventory built through the supply chain and is clearing through promotion, while demand rotates from legacy retro silhouettes toward newer performance product — exactly the exposure Foot Locker over-indexes to. The whole complex is drawn down together — [NKE](../NKE/NKE.md) at a 12-year low, [ONON](../ONON/ONON.md) –43% from its high, [LULU](../LULU/LULU.md) near multi-year lows — which argues the pressure is category-wide rather than DKS-specific execution failure, but also means no near-term catalyst ends it.

**Channel capacity is leaving.** JD Sports is closing ~175 Hibbett doors; DKS closed 110 Foot Locker doors in H1 and cut WSS from 143 to 99. A consolidating channel with shrinking square footage is the precondition for margin recovery in FY2028+, and scale survivors capture it.

E-commerce is now ~32% of sporting-goods sales (from 21% in 2020) — a headwind DKS partly neutralizes through store-based fulfillment (§2).

| **Analytical** | 🟢 **Strong** | The sum-of-the-parts sits in the company's own guidance table and the market is not doing it (§8). Requires holding two ideas at once — the good business is unimpaired *and* the bad one is genuinely bad. |
| **Informational** | 🟡 **Moderate** | DKS guides both segments separately and in detail, so there is little private information. The residual edge: Foot Locker's comps stay *proforma* and outside the reported comp base until Q4 FY2026, so most screens and models mis-handle the trajectory. |
| **Technical** | 🟢 **Moderate-Strong** | ~10th percentile of the 52-wk range, forced de-rating flows, 9.85% short float. Discounted by a 0.46 days-to-cover ratio — there is no squeeze mechanic, only depressed price. |

### BAIT Verdict: **Triple (B + A + T)**
The behavioral and analytical lenses reinforce each other unusually cleanly here — the crash *created* the analytical gap rather than revealing it. Compare [ONON](../ONON/ONON.md), where a similar "market misread a deliberate margin choice" setup carried founder buying three days after a 22% drop; DKS is the lower-quality business with the wider discount and a real balance-sheet-and-FCF complication ONON does not have.

## 11. Bull / Bear / Base Cases

*5-year terminal, FY2031E (fiscal year ending January 2032).*

### 🐂 Bull Case — $330 — Probability **20%**
Foot Locker works: Fast Break rolls the full fleet, the sneaker cycle turns, and Foot Locker returns to the ~5% margin it earned in 2021–22 on ~$8.0B (≈$400M). DICK'S compounds at ~4% comps plus new square footage to ~$18.5B at 11.5% (≈$2.13B); synergies exceed $125M; buybacks resume at depressed prices, taking shares to ~75M. FY2031E EBIT ≈$2.48B → EPS ≈**$23.50** at **14×**.

### 📊 Base Case — $215 — Probability **50%**
Foot Locker stops bleeding but never excels — ~1.5% margin on ~$7.5B (≈$113M) by FY2028, after the full charges and ~600 cumulative closures. DICK'S reaches ~$17.5B at 11.0% (≈$1.93B), the low end of its historical band, reflecting a permanently more promotional category. Capex normalizes toward $1.0–1.2B as the build-out matures, restoring FCF and funding buybacks to ~78M shares. FY2031E EBIT ≈$1.99B → EPS ≈**$17.70** at **12×**.

### 🐻 Bear Case — $100 — Probability **30%**
Foot Locker is structurally impaired: the fleet shrinks to ~1,400 doors and ~$5.5B at –1% margin, goodwill is written down, and lease obligations are paid regardless. The promotional cycle persists, DICK'S comps decelerate to ~+1% and margin compresses to 9.5% on ~$16B, and FCF stays below the dividend — halting buybacks and freezing dividend growth. FY2031E EPS ≈**$10.80** at a de-rated **9.5×**.

## 13. Recommendation & Bottom Line

**Thesis in one sentence**: The market repriced the whole of DICK'S for a problem confined to a segment that is ~34% of sales and none of its profit, leaving the unimpaired DICK'S business — still gaining share, its comp guidance untouched — at ≈8.3× its own guided EBIT, with Foot Locker, GameChanger and the Media Network thrown in below zero.

**For a non-holder**: 🟢 **Initiate (scaled)** — spot is ~35% below PW EV at the ~10th percentile of the 52-week range. Scale rather than commit at once: the promotional cycle is explicitly guided to worsen through Q3, so a better price is plausible.

**For a current holder**: 🟡 **Hold-Add on weakness** — nothing in Q2 impaired the DICK'S business, and the director cluster corroborates. Add on price, not on the Foot Locker story improving.

**Attractive entry zone**: **≤$163** (20% MoS to PW EV); high-conviction band **$120–150** — the 52-week low up to ~13× FY2026E.
**Trim zone**: **$204–330** · **Exit / avoid zone**: **≥$330** (the bull case, which requires Foot Locker at ~5% margin)

**Thesis-break triggers**:
- **DICK'S comp guidance is cut** — the guide surviving Q2 untouched is the load-bearing fact here; if it breaks, the sum-of-the-parts collapses
- **FY2026 FCF lands below the ~$450M dividend** with no FY2027 capex step-down — turns a capital-allocation concern into a balance-sheet one
- **Foot Locker's loss widens past the $(80)M floor**, or Q4's first non-proforma comp prints below the –2.0% guide
- **Goodwill impairment** against the $618M of Foot Locker goodwill — management conceding it is worth less than paid
- **Nike materially cuts allocation** or restarts an aggressive DTC shift
- **The $3.0B authorization goes unused through Q3** near these prices — confirms the Steward grade and removes the buyback leg of the base case

**Next review trigger**: the **Sept 14** Goldman fireside — whether a replacement Foot Locker profitability timeline appears and whether the unused authorization is addressed. Otherwise Q3 FY2026, ~2026-11-24.
