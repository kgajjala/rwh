## The Call

The market is pricing eBay as though the last four months did not happen. Spot is **−0.07%** against the last update — the stock round-tripped a live takeover bid *and* the best operating quarter in years, and ended where it started.

What it has wrong is the mix. The bear case was that Focus Categories were a rounding error dressed as a strategy and that Q1's reacceleration would fade into a guided 4–6pp deceleration. It did not arrive: Q2 GMV grew **14% FX-neutral**, management **raised** full-year guidance, and Focus, C2C and recommerce each grew over 20% to become **more than 70% of total volume**.

The one number that proves it: Focus Categories crossed **40% of GMV for the first time**, from roughly 30% a year ago — a ten-point mix shift toward the half that grows, inside a company returning **5.6%** of its market cap a year at 16.9× forward earnings.

The takeover is the free option, not the thesis. At $103.41 you are not paying for it.

## What I'd Have To Be Wrong About

- **Agentic commerce is a channel eBay can refuse.** The agent ban is a closed-platform bet that discovery stays on eBay's own surfaces. I'd look at organic traffic and long-tail GMV in the Q3 (**late Oct**) and Q4 prints; a decoupling where Focus Categories grow while total GMV stalls means the tail is being disintermediated and the ban accelerated it.
- **EU de minimis is absorbable.** The €150 exemption died **July 1, 2026** — Q3 is the first full quarter under it, with a second ~€2/parcel handling fee due November. I'd watch international FX-neutral GMV against the 10–12% Q3 guide; international is ≈51% of GMV, so a 3–4pp gap there is the whole guide.
- **GameStop is an option, not an overhang.** Cohen has neither closed nor withdrawn, and by August was weighing swapping the bid for a store partnership plus board seats. I'd look for a 13D/A or a formal withdrawal **by year-end**; withdrawal with no partnership leaves a 9.8% holder with no thesis and an exit to find.

Q2 answered the open question. Revenue **$3.1B (+15%, +14% FX-neutral)** on GMV **$22.4B**, GAAP net income **+51% to $552M**, non-GAAP operating margin **28.5%** — and management *raised* full-year FX-neutral GMV growth to 11.5–12.5%. The guided deceleration that framed the prior page did not show up.

The mix is the story. **Focus Categories grew 26% and passed 40% of GMV for the first time**, up from roughly 30%. US active buyers grew **6%** against 2% globally, and 16M enthusiast buyers spend ~$3,600 a year each. Advertising reached **$596M at 2.7% of GMV**, first-party ads +24% organic — still the margin lever with the most runway.

Two cautions. Q3 guidance implies revenue growing **8–10%** FX-neutral against GMV at **10–12%**, so take rate compresses at the margin. And FY2025's FCF step-down (**$1,956M → $1,434M**) has only partly mended. **Depop closed July 30 for $1.4B cash**, above the $1.2B announced, adding ~2.5pp to Q3 GMV growth while diluting EPS ~2.5pp until it turns accretive in 2028.

## 2. Moat & Competition

**Narrow, and genuinely widening.** The moat is not the marketplace — it is authentication plus liquidity in categories Amazon's first-party retail and Shopify DTC structurally cannot serve: one-off, used, graded and hard-to-value inventory. Over 15 million items authenticated, and by Iannone's account never a counterfeit through. Each new vertical reuses that same cost base, which is why the growth comes without margin giving way.

| Peer | Arena | Scale | Threat read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Whatnot** | Live collectibles | **$8B live GMV in 2025**, ~2× YoY; ~$11B valuation | Sharpest threat — attacks cards and collectibles, eBay's highest-margin vertical, in a format eBay has not scaled |
| **Amazon** | New-in-box retail | Dominant | Structurally mismatched to used/one-off; competes only at the commodity edge |
| **Fanatics / Topps** | Trading cards | Licence-holder | Vertical integration into a marketplace eBay does not own |
| **Vinted / Depop** | C2C fashion (EU) | Vinted ≈$6B GMV | Zero-fee buyer model pressures take rate; eBay now owns Depop and is inside this fight |
| **StockX / GOAT** | Sneakers, streetwear | Niche leaders | Authentication parity in one vertical; no breadth |
| **TikTok Shop** | Social/live | Scaling fast | Discovery-led, low-trust for high-value goods; a real threat only if authentication follows |

The window sharpened one competitive question: live commerce. eBay Live GMV grew **~8× YoY across seven markets** — but eBay declined to give the absolute number, which means it is almost certainly still small against Whatnot's $8B. Growth without a denominator is the tell.

The larger choice is **agentic commerce**, where eBay went the other way from the industry: it **banned autonomous third-party shopping agents** while building its own AI listing tools. That converts a disintermediation risk into a bet that buyers come to eBay for trust rather than to an agent for price — a defensible answer to the risk eBay itself named in the FY2025 10-K, and an unhedged one.

## 3. Management & Capital

**Jamie Iannone (CEO)** joined April 2020 and authored the Focus-Category strategy now carrying the thesis. **Peggy Alford (CFO)**, ex-PayPal, took the seat in May 2025 and owns the Depop integration and the 2028 accretion commitment.

**[Outsider grade](../../frameworks/outsiders.md) — Outsider** (unchanged). The cleanest case in the book: share count down ≈34% in six years through real net repurchases, bolt-on-only M&A, no debt-funded empire building. Q2 returned **$310M in buybacks plus $138M in dividends** — 4.4% buyback, 1.2% dividend. Depop at $1.4B is the largest cheque in years and still bolt-on against a $46B cap.

**Governance.** At the **June 17 annual meeting** — five weeks after the rejection — all 11 directors were elected, say-on-pay passed 323.5M to 43.6M, and a proposal to **lower the threshold for calling a special meeting failed 156.9M to 210.1M**: ~42.7% support for handing outside holders the exact lever Cohen would need.

**Appearances.** eBay files no shareholder letter, so substantive commentary sits in the Q2 call and one interview. Iannone spoke to **Fortune on August 6**, the day after the print, disclosing the AI-agent ban there rather than in any filing and quantifying the listing tool: *"When a user uses magical listings, we see 50% more listings per lister in the U.S."* Neither he nor Alford has addressed the takeover publicly since May; the Q2 release and call did not mention it at all.

**Insiders sold only.** CEO Iannone disposed of ~22,220 shares across August 5–6 at **$106.97–$113.23** under a 10b5-1 plan (~$2.4M); Rebecca Spencer sold 5,190 at ~$107.70 on August 10 **without** a plan. No officer or director bought.

**Multiples anchor.** At $46.0B cap / $49.9B EV: **16.9× FY26E EPS of $6.13** (15.4× FY27E $6.73), 4.2× EV/revenue, 16.1× EV/EBITDA, **5.3% FCF yield**. Worth stating plainly: **$125 in cash today would have beaten this**. A five-year path to $138 is ~6%/yr; $125 now, reinvested at almost anything, gets there sooner. What made the bid rejectable was not the number but that half of it was GameStop paper — precisely what the board said.

**Insiders** — sellers only; see §3. Nobody bought at a 17% discount to a live bid.

**BAIT — Single (A Strong).** Analytical carries it: the mix shift, US buyers +6% and a raised guide are all in the tape and none of it moved the price. Behavioral is Moderate — the round-trip of a takeover premium is suggestive, but at the 62nd percentile the stock is not washed out. Informational is Moderate and improving: the GameStop endgame and eBay Live's true scale are both unmodelled. Technical Moderate. Conviction: **Moderate-to-High**.
