## Business Overview

FedEx Corporation operates the world's largest express-transportation network: **~700 aircraft** (the largest all-cargo air fleet), **>175,000 vehicles**, **~440,000 employees**, **~17 million packages/day**, reaching **>220 countries and territories** and connecting **>99% of world GDP** ([FY2025 10-K](https://s21.q4cdn.com/665674268/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/FedEX-10K-Form.pdf)). On **June 1, 2024** FedEx merged FedEx Ground and FedEx Services into Federal Express Corporation — the "**One FedEx**" consolidation — collapsing two parallel pickup-and-delivery networks into one integrated air-ground footprint sorted into expedited / standard / economy tiers.

The defining event of FY2026 is **de-conglomeration**. After founder **Fred Smith died June 21, 2025** (age 80), CEO **Raj Subramaniam**'s "profit over volume" doctrine culminated in the **June 1, 2026 tax-free spin-off of FedEx Freight** (LTL) into standalone NYSE-listed **FDXF** (1 FDXF share per 2 FDX; FedEx retained 19.9%, to dispose within 24 months; ~$4.1B cash dividend to the parent). This page covers the **remaining FedEx** — a parcel/express pure-play. FedEx is also **changing its fiscal year-end from May 31 to December 31**, effective June 1, 2026; forward "FY2027" guidance is on the new calendar-aligned basis.

## Pivotal Investment Question

> Can Subramaniam's de-conglomeration (Freight spin) plus DRIVE / Network 2.0 cost-out lift the parcel RemainCo's returns above its cost of capital — re-rating FedEx from a chronically sub-WACC cyclical carrier (ROIC ~6.7%) to a disciplined ~8%-margin parcel compounder by FY2029 — *before* Amazon insourcing and the secular decline of high-margin Express/air freight hollow out the core?

### Primary Source: 10-K Segment Detail (FY2025)

The FY2025 10-K shows the structural problem DRIVE attacks: revenue was flat ($87.9B) but **operating income fell** on (i) the **USPS air-cargo contract loss** (lost to UPS Sept 2024 → U.S. average daily freight pounds −44%), (ii) a **mix shift toward lower-yield deferred/economy services** that "constrained yield growth," and (iii) higher purchased-transportation and wage rates — partly offset by **$2.2B of DRIVE structural savings**. The single clearest moat-erosion signal sits in FedEx's own FY2025 volume table: **international priority −12%, U.S. priority −2%**, while **international economy +40%** and U.S. deferred/ground grew — the highest-margin premium-air business is in secular decline as growth concentrates in commoditized deferred lanes. The entire Tricolor air-network redesign and Network 2.0 surface consolidation are responses to this. ## Section 2 — Revenue Mix & Geographic Split

### Segment / Service Structure (post-spin)

| Segment | Status | Characteristic |
|---|---|---|
| **Federal Express** (Express + Ground + Services, unified June 2024) | The pure-play | Integrated air-ground parcel; services reorganized into **expedited / standard / economy**; FY2026 segment revenue +9%, adj op income **+17%**, margin +60 bps |
| ~~FedEx Freight (LTL)~~ | **Spun off June 1, 2026 → FDXF** | No longer consolidated after Q4 FY2026; FedEx retains 19.9% (to dispose ≤24 mo) |

**Service-mix shift** is the model's central tension: e-commerce drives volume into **low-yield deferred/economy/residential**, while the **premium overnight/priority and B2B document** business (FedEx's historical profit core) structurally shrinks. "Profit over volume" — pricing and yield discipline over chasing parcels — is the explicit strategic response. International export package **yield +4% in FY2026**, partly *because* low-yield China de-minimis lightweight volume fell away.

### Geographic / Lane Note

The **China→US lane is ~2.5% of consolidated revenue and FedEx's most profitable intercontinental lane** ([Q4 FY25 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2025/06/25/fedex-fdx-fy2025-q4-earnings-call-transcript/)). Its disruption by de-minimis removal (see §6/§7) drove a ~$1B FY2026 trade headwind. Subramaniam frames the broader shift as "**re-globalization**" — China-US declining while intra-Asia and Asia-Europe lanes grow, where FedEx is re-pointing capacity (new Singapore→Anchorage route, Asia-to-Europe shift).

## Section 3 — Competitive Moat & Landscape

**Rating: Narrow — and eroding at the edges.** FedEx's moat is genuine: the **world's largest all-cargo air fleet** (~700 aircraft vs. UPS Airlines ~290, DHL Aviation ~300, Amazon Air ~110), a hub-and-spoke system anchored by the **Memphis superhub** (42 miles of conveyor), unmatched air route authorities, customs expertise, and a network the 10-K calls "extremely difficult, costly, and time-consuming to replicate." But the moat **protects share, not excess returns**: ROIC (~6.7%) has chronically sat *below* the cost of capital — a structurally wide-moat business should not under-earn its WACC for a decade.

### Moat Sources

| Source | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| World's largest air cargo fleet + route authorities | Strong | ~700 aircraft; >220 countries; connects >99% world GDP; overnight to ~95% of US population |
| Hub-and-spoke density (Memphis, Indianapolis, Paris, Guangzhou) | Strong | Decades of capex; irreplaceable airport slots/infrastructure |
| Brand + entrenched enterprise contracts | Moderate | Healthcare, aerospace, high-tech; switching friction at the enterprise tier |
| Scale economics (fuel, procurement, customs) | Moderate | But offset by asset intensity → sub-WACC returns |
| **RLA labor structure** | Moderate | Post-"One FedEx," ~440K US employees fall under the Railway Labor Act (nationwide-class organizing + mandatory NMB mediation before strike); only ~1% unionized (pilots) vs. **UPS Teamsters >half** under the NLRA — a structural cost edge ([FY2025 10-K](https://s21.q4cdn.com/665674268/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/Annual-Report.pdf)) |

### Competitive Landscape

US parcel market 2025: total **23.1B parcels (+3.3%)** ([2025 Pitney Bowes Parcel Shipping Index](https://www.investorrelations.pitneybowes.com/news-releases/news-release-details/pitney-bowes-parcel-shipping-index-us-carrier-disruption)).

| Competitor | Scale / Share | Threat Vector | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Amazon Logistics** | **#1 US carrier by volume (~6.7–6.9B parcels)**; insources >⅔ of own deliveries; Amazon Air ~110 planes | Removes the single largest structural volume pool; building LTL + 3P ambitions | **Highest-secular-threat peer.** FedEx exited Amazon in 2019, **re-engaged Feb 2025** for big-and-bulky residential (yield-accretive). Amazon lacks a time-definite *global express* network — that's FedEx's defensible ground |
| **UPS** | ~31.6% US revenue share (down from 34%); ~4.4B parcels; fwd P/E ~14× | The direct duopoly peer; integrated air-ground; won the USPS contract | Both now run "profit over volume"; **UPS cutting Amazon volume >50% by 2H 2026.** UPS is Teamsters-unionized (cost ↑); FedEx largely non-union = structural cost edge |
| **DHL / DP-DHL** | Global #1 international express; dominant in Europe | International express overlap | Strongest outside the US; less direct in US domestic |
| **USPS** | ~6.6B parcels; universal-service ground/last-mile | Subsidized lightweight residential; buys air lift (now from UPS) | A subsidized competitor and *former* FedEx air customer — **not** a FedEx last-mile dependency |
| **Regional ("Other": OnTrac, etc.)** | Doubled to **7.2%** revenue share (from 3.4%) | Low-cost lightweight residential share gain | Fast-growing nibble at the low-yield edge |

**How FedEx's moat differs**: the integrated **global time-definite express** network (air + ground + customs to 220+ countries) is the one thing Amazon, regionals, and USPS cannot replicate — it is decades of slot, aircraft, and hub investment. **Tail-risk read**: that core is also the *slowest-growing, highest-margin* part of the business in secular decline, while all the volume growth is in commoditized deferred/residential lanes where Amazon (now #1 by volume) and regionals compete hardest and FedEx's asset base is a disadvantage. The moat is real but narrowing; the bull case requires cost-out to outrun mix erosion.

## Section 4 — Management & Leadership

### CEO: Raj Subramaniam (since June 1, 2022)

Only the second CEO in FedEx history. Joined 1991 as a marketing analyst; ran FedEx Express (2018) and was COO before becoming President & CEO. His tenure *is* the thesis: a deliberate break from founder Fred Smith's "empire builder" model toward "**profit over volume**" — DRIVE, One FedEx, Network 2.0, the Tricolor air redesign, and the Freight spin-off. ([Fortune profile](https://fortune.com/article/fedex-ceo-raj-subramaniam-fred-smith-globalization-trade-tariffs/))

### Governance & transition risk ⚠️
- **Founder Fred Smith died June 21, 2025** (age 80); **R. Brad Martin** (ex-Saks CEO) elected Chairman June 23, 2025; board reduced to 12. ([FedEx obituary](https://newsroom.fedex.com/newsroom/global-english/frederick-w-smith-visionary-founder-of-fedex-dies-at-80))
- **CFO John Dietrich stepped down June 1, 2026** (through July 31) on spin completion; **interim CFO Claude Russ** (24-yr FedEx veteran, ex-FedEx Freight CFO); permanent search ongoing. The CFO vacancy lands right after the Feb-2026 Investor Day set FY2029 targets — a mild execution-credibility flag.
- COO **Scott Ray** (U.S./Canada surface ops) effective June 1, 2026.

**[Outsider grade](../../frameworks/outsiders.md) — Reinvestor (reforming).** History weighs against an Outsider grade: the **2016 TNT Express acquisition (~$4.8B)** was a value-destructive integration (NotPetya cyberattack, European drag), and **ROIC has chronically trailed WACC** (~4.4% FY20 → ~6.7% TTM). But Subramaniam is pivoting hard toward discipline — the Freight spin is a genuine value-unlock, capex intensity is at a **company-low 4.0% of revenue**, and capital returns continue. The pivot is real but unproven; the grade upgrades only when ROIC clears WACC.

**Capital allocation**:

## Section 5 — Strategic Growth Initiatives

1. **Network 2.0** — fold legacy Express (530 facilities) + Ground (720 facilities) surface networks into one; ~290 locations converted by May 2025 (Canada complete); ~30% of package facilities to close; **$2B savings target by FY2027** — the live cost lever.
2. **DRIVE (complete)** — $4.0B permanent structural cost-out achieved FY2025 ($1.8B FY24 + $2.2B FY25); a further >$1B transformation savings in FY2026.
3. **Tricolor air-network redesign** — match aircraft/lanes to demand; re-point capacity from China-US to Asia-Europe (Singapore→Anchorage 6×/week).
4. **Freight spin-off (done)** — surfaces the high-multiple LTL business and lets the parcel RemainCo be valued on its own returns.
5. **Margin path to FY2029** (Feb-2026 Investor Day, ex-Freight): ~$98B revenue, **~$8B operating income, ~8% margin, $6B FCF** — the explicit re-rating anchor.
6. **Yield management** — "profit over volume": shedding low-yield e-commerce parcels, raising international export yield (+4% FY2026).

## Section 7 — Industry-Specific Macro Analysis

- **Freight recession bottomed; early-cycle recovery underway.** The 2023–2025 freight recession (Cass Truckload Linehaul −10% in 2023, −3.4% in 2024) turned: **Cass +1.8% in 2025**, ATA tonnage **+3.0% YoY (March 2026)**, capacity tightening into 2026 ([Cass April 2026](https://www.cassinfo.com/freight-audit-payment/cass-transportation-indexes/april-2026)). A cyclical tailwind for FedEx volumes — and upside optionality, since FY2026's record print came *with* a still-soft macro.
- **China de-minimis removal**: the Section 321 ($800 duty-free) exemption ended for China/HK **May 2, 2025** ([White House EO 14256 fact sheet](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/04/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-closes-de-minimis-exemptions-to-combat-chinas-role-in-americas-synthetic-opioid-crisis/)) and **globally Aug 29, 2025** ([EO 14324](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/07/suspending-duty-free-de-minimis-treatment-for-all-countries/)). De-minimis had grown to ~4M packages/day (~1.36B in 2024); China-US daily volume fell ~85% post-May; China→US e-commerce air exports −51%/−52% YoY (Oct/Nov 2025). FedEx absorbed a **~$1B FY2026 adj-operating-income headwind** (mostly top-line China-US revenue + ~$300M direct trade/customs expense) yet still grew EPS 11%.
- **TAM**: global parcel market **~$500–528B / ~435B packages in 2026** (~5.6% CAGR); US parcel 23.1B parcels (+3.3%, forecast +36% by 2030, Pitney Bowes). Structurally, low-yield e-commerce parcels grow secularly while higher-yield industrial/B2B freight is cyclical — FedEx's "profit over volume" pivot tries to optimize that mix rather than chase the low-yield tail.
- **Regulatory**: trade/tariff volatility is the persistent international-volume swing factor; IC-classification and labor are domestic policy tail risks.
