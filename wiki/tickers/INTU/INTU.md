## Business Overview

Intuit (NASDAQ: INTU) is a vertical SaaS platform serving consumers, small and mid-market businesses, and accountants across four product lines: **QuickBooks** (de-facto SMB accounting standard, ≈34% share; 8.9M online paying customers), **TurboTax** (≈70% of U.S. DIY e-filed returns; 39.0M U.S. units in FY26), **Credit Karma** (100M+ member financial-product marketplace, acquired 2020 for $8.1B), and **Mailchimp** (acquired 2021 for $12B). FY2026 revenue was **$21.448B (+14%)** with a 27.4% GAAP operating margin and $8.838B of operating cash flow. (*[Q4 FY26 press release, Aug 25, 2026](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1320/intuit-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-fiscal-2026-results-sets-fiscal-2027-guidance)*)

The company positions itself as an **"AI-driven expert platform"** — pairing AI with human expertise (TurboTax Live, IES accountants) rather than competing with horizontal LLMs on raw capability. FY2026 forced a strategic concession to that framing: after admitting in May that TurboTax "lost on price" among sub-$50K DIY filers, Intuit entered FY2027 **deliberately lowering entry-level DIY prices and accepting lower initial ARPC to rebuild volume**, and launched **QuickBooks Free and Lite** to widen the SMB funnel. It also began **distributing TurboTax through "leading LLM experiences"** — converting the frontier-lab threat that drove the June de-rating into a distribution channel. Two accounting changes accompany the reset: SBC enters non-GAAP from FY2027, and **Mailchimp becomes a separate reportable segment** (guided **−1% to 0%**), formally isolating the $12B acquisition as a run-off cash asset. (*[Q4 FY26 call, Aug 25, 2026](https://uk.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/earnings-call-transcript-intuit-beats-q4-2026-estimates-but-shares-fall-on-softer-outlook-93CH-4846801)*)

## Pivotal Investment Question

> Is the FY2027 reset — 9–10% revenue, TurboTax priced down to +2–3%, a long-term GBS algorithm cut to 10–15% — the deliberate bottom of a J-curve that buys back customer growth, or the first hard evidence that AI-native and low-cost competitors are structurally capping a wide-moat franchise's terminal growth?

Everything turns on the two customer numbers: **QuickBooks online paying customers +3%** and **TurboTax units −2%**. If Goodarzi is right that "*we are creating the pressure, we are not being pressured*," cheaper entry points plus LLM distribution rebuild both funnels and the franchise re-accelerates to low-double-digits by FY2029 — the base case. If BofA is right that TurboTax "is losing market share to lower-cost AI alternatives," the price cuts are a defensive toll that permanently resets both growth and margin, and 15× FY27E is not cheap enough.

## Section 3 — Competitive Moat & Landscape

**Moat rating: Wide, narrowing** — the switching costs and accountant network are intact, but FY2026 was the first year that *pricing power at the bottom of both funnels* visibly failed.

| Moat Source | Strength | Evidence |
|-------------|----------|----------|
| Switching costs (QBO payroll + tax + payments) | Strong | Accounting data, payroll records, bank feeds and filing history create high migration friction; Online Accounting revenue still +23% in FY26 |
| Accountant network effect | Strong | ≈600K accountants trained on QuickBooks; ≈3/4 of mid-market adds come from upgrades/desktop migrations inside the installed base |
| TurboTax brand | **Moderate-Strong** *(downgraded)* | Still ≈70% of U.S. DIY e-file, but management says **"price is now the number one reason customers leave TurboTax"** |
| Credit Karma data flywheel | Moderate-Strong | 100M+ members; dual TurboTax + Credit Karma customers monetize at ≈2× |
| Proprietary data isolation (AI moat) | Moderate | Contractual walling of Intuit data from partner models; **75%+ of IES customers use Intuit AI agents monthly** — the strongest evidence agentic AI is a moat asset rather than a threat |

**Moat vulnerabilities**:
- **Price is now the stated #1 churn reason in TurboTax** — and Intuit's answer is to cut price, which caps Consumer revenue at +2–3% and concedes the low end permanently unless volume follows.
- **The QuickBooks funnel has stalled** (§2). This is new, and it is the vulnerability that matters most: it moves the AI/low-cost threat from tax, where it was expected, into the SMB core, where it was not. JPMorgan's downgrade cited exactly this.
- Horizontal LLMs continue to compress willingness to pay for the non-expert tier — though Intuit is now **distributing TurboTax inside LLM experiences**, partially converting the threat into a channel.
- **Mailchimp** has lost ground to Klaviyo and HubSpot; now isolated as a separate segment at −1% to 0%.

## Section 5 — Strategic Growth Initiatives

1. **Funnel reconstruction (the FY2027 bet)**: QuickBooks **Free and Lite** plus lower TurboTax entry pricing, aimed squarely at the +3% / −2% customer problem. Early proof point: **>20,000 QuickBooks Free users or paid converts in the first month**. The monetization path is payments, payroll, and later upgrades — which is why FY27 revenue growth is guided down while customer growth is meant to go up. This is the single initiative the next four quarters will be judged on.

2. **Mid-market wedge (IES + QBO Advanced)**: **IES annualized revenue >$145M, 4× YoY**; mid-market revenue +39% in Q4; customers +28%; new-to-franchise mid-market +30%. Against NetSuite this is still small, but it is the fastest-compounding line in the company and the main lever that turns a 10–15% GBS algorithm into the top of that range.

3. **Agentic AI inside the paid tiers**: **75%+ of IES customers use Intuit's AI agents monthly** — agents are already habitual for the highest-ARPU cohort. Goodarzi's "*unified financial system of intelligence... increasingly does the work for our customers*" is the direction; the monetization is indirect today (retention, cost-to-serve, upsell routing).
