## The Call

The market prices Kodiak at **10.2× this year's EBITDA**, a turn above Archrock and USA Compression, and at a steady-state cash-flow yield that implies about **1.5% growth forever** at a 10% discount rate. It is paying for the compression franchise and giving the power build roughly nothing net of what it costs to fund.

On the funding it is right to be careful. Free cash flow was **−$87.5M in Q2**; 2026 capex of $805–895M runs against ~$585M of discretionary cash flow, and 2027 needs roughly $1.5B (400 MW at $2.7M/MW plus $300M of compressors) against perhaps $650M. May's $836M raise at $71 covered this year; the next $3B or so arrives with the ten-year Treasury at 5% and the stock at $58.

On the business it is too cautious. Compression revenue per horsepower rose **4.5%** at **98.2% utilization** with new engines 200 weeks out, and the compression business alone compounds discretionary cash flow per share at roughly 8–9% plus a 3.4% yield. Power is real but unproven at scale: 1.8 GW of equipment secured at fixed prices, management standing behind **15%-plus unlevered returns and five-year paybacks**, and a first hyperscaler project at limited notice to proceed but not yet under contract.

The number that decides the verb is the hurdle. At a flat multiple the Base compounds near 13%; weighted for a one-in-four Bear and a terminal multiple at peer level once the build is done, the PW return is **10.9%**. The shortfall is about $5 of price. Entry is $53, or a signed anchor at rates consistent with those returns.

## What I'd Have To Be Wrong About

1. **That the anchor is real.** Test: a signed contract of ten years or longer for the West Texas project, announced by the Q4 print (February 2027), at pricing consistent with a 15% unlevered return on $2.7M/MW, which means roughly $50K per MW-month or more. No signature by then, or a seven-year deal at the legacy fleet's ~$30K rate, and the 2 GW plan is a pipeline, not a business.
2. **That the build stays inside the balance sheet.** Test: credit-agreement leverage at or below 3.5× at both the Nov 3 and February prints, a FY2027 capex plan funded from discretionary cash flow, the ABL and notes, and no second equity raise below May's $71. A breach means the "peak in the mid-3s" promise failed inside a year.
3. **That compression pricing holds at peak utilization.** Test: Q3 and Q4 revenue per horsepower up at least 3.5% year over year (≥$24.40 by Q4) with utilization ≥97.5% and compression adjusted gross margin ≥69% despite the lube-oil headwind. Below that, 70% was the peak margin, and the Base's 3%-a-year price line is too high.

## 1. Business & Numbers

Kodiak owns and operates large-horsepower gas compression under multi-year take-or-pay contracts, mostly in the Permian and Eagle Ford, and since April 2026 a behind-the-meter power fleet (recips and turbines) at data centers, microgrids and industrial sites. Three segments: **Compression Infrastructure** (81% of Q2 revenue), **Power Infrastructure** (8%) and **Other Services** (11%, station construction and overhauls).

What changed: Power Infrastructure's first full quarter, a second consecutive 70% compression margin on a price increase, a guidance raise, and explicit 2030 targets of **5.2M horsepower and 2 GW**.
