## The Call

Two quarters resolved most of the ambiguity, and not favourably. Americas comps went **−5% → −12%**. More damaging, **International rolled over** — +13% comps in Q1 to **−3%** in Q2 (+8% → −6% constant currency). The growth engine this thesis leaned on is gone.

The guidance cut is worse than it reads. June's $10.95–11.15 EPS guide explicitly **excluded** tariff refunds; September's $9.48–9.73 guide **includes $0.86** of them. Strip that out and the underlying guide is ≈**$8.75 — a −21% cut**, not −13%. The same $134.5M refund flattered Q2's gross margin by **560 bps**; ex-refund, margin fell ~360 bps and EPS was ≈$2.06 against $3.10 a year ago. Q3 is guided to **−10% to −11% revenue**, worse than Q2's −4%.

What survives is genuinely cheap: **≈11.5× clean earnings**, $1.4B net cash, no debt, a 60.5% gross margin and inventory units down 7%. And in four days Heidi O'Neill takes over. This is a real brand at a distressed multiple — but nothing yet says the decline has found a floor.

## What I'd Have To Be Wrong About

1. **That the brand is impaired rather than mismanaged.** Test: Q3 (early December) International comps. A brand losing customers declines everywhere; a company executing badly declines where it is executing badly. International returning to positive constant-currency comps would say this is fixable; a second negative quarter says the problem followed LULU overseas.
2. **That O'Neill inherits a floor rather than a falling knife.** Test: her first guidance, most likely at Q3. New CEOs routinely rebase. BofA has already flagged that her arrival *"could cause further strategic changes that may lead to another round of guidance reductions"* — a kitchen-sink reset would be healthy long-term and brutal near-term, and my Base case assumes a trough in FY2027, not FY2028.
3. **That the clean multiple (§4) is a floor rather than a waypoint.** JPMorgan's post-print target is **$95 — below spot**. If the Street is right that earnings power is still falling, a low multiple on falling earnings is a value trap, not a discount.

## 2. Moat & Competition

**Narrow, and eroding.** The brand still commands a **60.5% gross margin** and a community built on technical fabric IP, the ambassador model and a vertical DTC channel that captures full retail price. That is a real moat — but a moat is meant to defend share, and LULU is losing share at home while still charging premium prices.

What would break it entirely: the fabric IP has no patent life that matters, the ambassador model is replicable, and premium positioning survives only while the customer accepts the price. Markdown discipline is the leading indicator — Q1's 410 bps gross-margin decline was the first crack, and Q2's underlying decline confirmed it.

| Competitor | Position | Threat |
|---|---|---|
| **Vuori** | Private, fast-growing US premium athleisure | Taking exactly LULU's premium customer, with newer product and no legacy store base. Private, so it can fund share gains without quarterly scrutiny. |
| **Alo Yoga** | Private, premium women's studio-to-street | Head-on in LULU's founding category with stronger current brand heat. |
| **Nike** | Global scale; O'Neill's former employer | Also at a multi-year low ([NKE](../NKE/NKE.md)) — the whole premium athletic complex is de-rating, which argues category, not company-specific. |
| **On Holding** | Premium performance, running-led | Growing where LULU is shrinking; see [ONON](../ONON/ONON.md). Evidence that premium pricing still works when the product is right. |

**Industry**: premium athleisure is post-boom. The 2020–2023 expansion pulled demand forward and drew in well-funded private entrants competing on newness rather than price. The category did not die — [ONON](../ONON/ONON.md) is still growing — LULU simply stopped being the newest thing in it.

## 3. Management & Capital

**The interim period ends this week.** Calvin McDonald departed as CEO on January 31, 2026. **Meghan Frank** (CFO) and **André Maestrini** (CCO) have run the company as **interim Co-CEOs** since, with **Marti Morfitt** as executive chair — a seven-month vacuum during which comps deteriorated in both regions. 📅 **Heidi O'Neill becomes CEO and joins the board on September 8, 2026**: a 30-year industry veteran, most recently President of Consumer, Product & Brand at Nike. Her appointment (announced April 22) was met with a ~4% share decline; the market has not yet given her the benefit of the doubt.

✅ **The governance overhang is resolved.** The Chip Wilson proxy fight ended in a **Cooperation Agreement dated May 26, 2026**, under which **Laura Gentile and Marc Maurer** joined the board after the June 25 annual meeting, expanding it from 9 to 11. All three Class I directors were elected. ⚠️ One warning sign survived the settlement: say-on-pay drew **27.0M votes against versus 46.4M for — a 36.8% rebuke**, unusually high for an uncontested advisory vote.

💰 **Outsider grade: Anti-Outsider.** This is the clearest capital-destruction record on the wiki. Buybacks peaked at **$1,637M in FY2024** with the stock in the $300s–$400s, ran $1,178M in FY2025, and continued at **$688M through H1 FY2026 at a ≈$140 average** — 40% above today's price. Share count has fallen only 9% in five years for roughly $4.6B spent. The company bought most aggressively at the top and is still buying into a decline it did not forecast. 🟢 The one countervailing signal: director **Charles Bergh** bought **4,275 shares at $117.05 on June 16** (~$500K, Form 4 code P) — the sole open-market purchase in the window, and already ~14% underwater.

**Appearances (May 1 – Sep 4)** — the [Q1 call](../../../raw/LULU/press-releases/2026-06-04_Q1-FY26_earnings.txt) (Jun 4) and the [Q2 call](../../../raw/LULU/press-releases/2026-09-03_Q2-FY26_earnings.txt) (Sep 3). No conference fireside, television interview or podcast located — unsurprising with two interim Co-CEOs, and itself a signal: **there has been no one to tell the story for seven months.**
