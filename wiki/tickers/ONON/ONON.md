## Business Overview

On Holding AG (NYSE: ONON) is a Swiss premium athletic and lifestyle brand founded in Zurich in 2010 by Caspar Coppetti, David Allemann, and former Ironman triathlete Olivier Bernhard. The founding insight — that a garden-hose-prototype cushioning system could combine performance with Swiss design aesthetics — evolved into the proprietary CloudTec sole architecture that defines On's visual identity. The brand competes in the $140B global athletic footwear market anchored on premium positioning: no permanent discounting, controlled retail distribution, and an elite athlete ecosystem headlined by co-investor Roger Federer, Grand Slam champion Iga Świątek, and Tour players Ben Shelton and Jack Draper.

**FY2025 scale**: CHF 3.0B in net sales (+30% reported, **+35.6% constant currency**), record full-year gross margin of **62.8%**, adj. EBITDA margin of ≈18.8%, and year-end cash of CHF 1.02B (highest in company history). The DTC channel — on-running.com plus 67 owned stores — contributed **41.8%** of net sales (up 110bps YoY), with over 60% of apparel and accessories revenue flowing through DTC. Revenue is reported in three geographies: Americas (U.S.-led, largest), EMEA (brand origin market), and APAC (fastest growth — surpassed CHF 1.0B in FY2025). Key growth levers beyond footwear include apparel (up 46% c-c in Q4 2025, growing from 5% to a target ≈15% of mix), APAC expansion (China + Japan + Korea retail buildout), and the Cyclon subscription shoe concept.

## Pivotal Investment Question

> When On cut its own wholesale shipments to avoid discounting, was that a premium brand exercising pricing power — or a brand discovering it no longer has the demand to fill the channel?

Everything turns on this. If it is discipline, On has just done deliberately and early what Nike spent three years failing to do, and the reward is a structurally higher-margin business entering a 2027 product cycle with a clean channel. If it is demand, then "protecting full-price integrity" is a euphemism, the 2027 pipeline is a deferral, and the fashion-cycle bears are right about a brand at peak penetration. The disclosed numbers are genuinely split: **gross margin rose to a record 65.4% and the margin guide went up**, which a brand losing pricing power cannot do — but **inventory grew 31% against 13.5% reported sales growth**, which a brand in full control of its channel should not need. Q3 resolves it.

### The Q2 2026 Wholesale Decision — Discipline or Demand?

This is the pivotal question (above) in operational form, and it deserves the evidence laid out rather than summarised.

**What On did.** Wholesale net sales grew just **+12.7% c-c** in Q2, against **+25.1% c-c** in Q1 — roughly halved in a single quarter. Management states this was deliberate: sell-in was cut to protect full-price integrity, concentrated in the **US market**, in response to what CFO Frank Sluis called a "highly promotional multi-brand marketplace." The stated purpose is to enter 2027 with clean channel inventory ahead of a product cycle that includes Cloudsurfer 3, Cloudsurfer Max 2 and a Cloudflow relaunch — a cycle On had already walked its top-100 wholesale partners through in Paris six weeks earlier (§9). Sluis confirmed the revenue guide cut is "indeed linked to wholesale actions taken in Q2 and Q3," and guided Q3 growth below Q4 as the actions continue.

**What management said.** Sluis: *"We do not compromise our full-price integrity for volume – even in the heavily promotional environment we saw this quarter in some markets."* Co-CEO Caspar Coppetti on the price architecture — $160 entry, $200–210 mid, $300+ premium — added that *"lowering prices is not part of the premium brand's playbook,"* citing strong demand at the top of the range (Cloudmonster Hyper, LightSpray at up to $290). Co-CEO David Allemann framed the quarter as proof *"that a brand can achieve global scale without compromising its premium brand positioning."*

**Evidence the decision is genuine discipline:**
- **The wholesale partners bought *more*, not less.** At On's inaugural [On Running Summit](https://press.on-running.com/on-unveils-next-era-of-performance-innovation-and-reveals-sensation-first-run-brand-identity) in Paris (June 26–28), the company previewed the SS27 collection to its **top 100 global run-specialty partners** — and CFO Frank Sluis disclosed on the Q2 call that **Spring/Summer 2027 orders for the Cloudsurfer 3 doubled from those retailers after the event**. This is the single most discriminating datapoint available before Q3: the same channel whose Q2 sell-in was cut placed double the forward orders on the product the cut was made for. A channel rejecting the brand does not do that. It does not prove sell-out recovers, but it does establish that the sell-in reduction was a timing decision the retailers were party to, not a demand verdict imposed on On.
- **Gross margin went the wrong way for the bear case.** A brand losing pricing power discounts to clear, and margin compresses. On's expanded 390bps to a record 65.4%, *and* the full-year GM guide was raised in the same release that cut revenue. That combination is very difficult to fake.
- **DTC accelerated as wholesale decelerated.** +34.3% c-c against +12.7%. If the *brand* were weakening, the channel On fully controls — no retailer intermediation, no promotional pressure — would weaken too. It did the opposite, to a record 45.7% mix.
- **The weakness is geographically contained.** APAC +54.7% c-c, EMEA +20.5%. Only the Americas, where the US multi-brand promotional environment lives, decelerated sharply.
- **On can afford it.** Record CHF 1.21B cash, 19.8% EBITDA margin. Choosing margin over volume is a luxury available to a balance sheet like this one and not to a stressed competitor.

**Evidence it is at least partly demand:**
- **The stated trigger was sell-out, not sell-in.** Management attributed the action to *"softer sell-out in some everyday running franchises"* — i.e. consumers buying less at retail. Cutting shipments in response to soft consumer purchase is a *consequence* of demand weakness, correctly managed. It is not the same thing as choosing restraint from a position of excess demand.
- **Inventory +31% YoY** against +13.5% reported sales growth. A brand in full command of its channel does not accumulate inventory at 2.3× its sales growth rate. Management's "primarily volume-led" explanation is consistent with pre-building for 2027, and equally consistent with product not moving.
- **"Everyday running" is the core, not the fringe.** The softness is in the volume franchises that carry the brand, not in an experimental line.
- **The 2027 pipeline is a promise, not a result.** Deferring the payoff to an unlaunched product cycle is the oldest move in consumer brands, and it is unfalsifiable until it ships.

**The honest read**: both are true, and they are not contradictory. US everyday-running demand genuinely softened in a promotional market; On responded by protecting price instead of volume. That is the correct response, and it is a materially better position than Nike occupied in 2022–2023, when the same channel dynamics were met with discounting (see [NKE §3](../NKE/NKE.md)). The doubled SS27 order book materially raises the odds this was the former, because it is the one piece of evidence that comes from the counterparties rather than from management. What On has *not* yet demonstrated is that the trade pays off at the *consumer* level — retailer orders are a bet on sell-out, not sell-out itself — and the discriminating evidence is specific and arrives in one quarter: **does gross margin hold ≥65% while inventory growth converges toward sales growth?** If yes, this was discipline and the 2027 cycle launches into a clean channel. If margin slips or inventory stays 25%+ above sales growth, the discipline framing was cover for demand deterioration and the bear case is live.

**Honest tail-risk competitive read**:

## Section 5 — Strategic Growth Initiatives

The customer-acquisition signal (6% → 10% of new customers acquired through apparel) is the leading indicator that apparel is a top-of-funnel, not just upsell, play.

2. **APAC buildout (highest-growth region, CHF 1B+ FY2025)**: China + Japan + Korea retail expansion. China Tmall top-5 in $140+ footwear; China in-store traffic doubled. APAC grew +85% c-c in Q4 2025. This is the most underpenetrated large market — APAC at ≈17% of revenue vs. Americas ≈58% — and the one with the longest growth runway.

3. **DTC mix shift (target: >45% by 2027E)**: Continued shift from wholesale to own.com + owned retail. Each 1pt gain in DTC mix = ≈30bps gross margin *[Estimate]*. 67 stores planned to expand. DTC gives superior customer data for targeting, product development feedback, and inventory management.

4. **Tennis platform**: Świątek (#1 WTA) + Shelton (top-10 ATP) + Draper give ONON credible court presence. Tennis-shoe SKUs (The Roger Clubhouse, etc.) create premium lifestyle crossover. The on-court category is small but carries outsized brand-signal value relative to revenue.

5. **The 2027 innovation cycle — now the thesis's load-bearing initiative**: management explicitly cut FY2026 wholesale sell-in to clear the channel for it (§3), which converts the pipeline from optionality into obligation. The cycle is closer than "2027" implies: Allemann confirmed On *"will begin scaling the Cloudsurfer 3, our first shoe with our new Surreal superfoam, in **October** with Run Specialty Partners"* — i.e. this year, into the channel just cleared. Coppetti committed on the same call that **all everyday-running franchises update to new foams and technologies within 14 months**. Rollouts: **Cloudsurfer 3, Cloudsurfer Max 2, and a Cloudflow relaunch**, refreshing the franchises where sell-out softened. New technologies: **CloudTec Steel**, **Helion HF superfoam**, **Surreal foam**, with Cloudboom Strike 2 claiming a 1.6% running-economy improvement. Q3 2026 already carries Cloud X 5 and Cloudrunner Max. Alongside these: LightSpray (3D-sprayed uppers), Cyclon (subscription/circular concept), and Cloudwander (hiking crossover). The October Cloudsurfer 3 scaling means the first read arrives at Q3, not in 2027.

6. **Hiking + outdoor lifestyle**: Cloudwander + Cloudaway + Cloudventure positioned in trail/hike segment. The Hoka-vs-On battle for trail running premium is still open. APAC mountain culture (Japan, Korea, China) gives ONON a natural trial base for trail/hike.

7. **Football expansion**: Co-founder Olivier Bernhard told Bloomberg (June 2026, interview) that On plans to expand into football — aligns with JP Morgan's "more multi-dimensional growth strategy" framing. No formal partnership announced.

8. **CleanCloud sustainability**: Commercially launched CleanCloud midsole in Cloud X 5 (July 2026) — first commercially scaled sportswear midsole made from captured carbon emissions (Infinium eNaphtha supply). Sustainability differentiation for premium brand narrative.

## Section 7 — Industry-Specific Macro Analysis

**Athletic footwear TAM**: ≈$140B globally *[Analyst consensus]*; premium segment ($100–300 retail price) growing faster than mass. ONON has <2% global share — substantial raw runway, though premium-segment share is meaningfully higher.

**Running + trail premium sub-segment**: $15–25B global *[Estimate]*. The most attractive sub-segment for ONON — over-indexed relative to total footwear. Nike (Vaporfly + Pegasus), Hoka (Clifton + Bondi + Mafate), ONON (Cloud + Cloudsurfer + Cloudmonster), Adidas (Adizero + Ultraboost), Asics (Nimbus + Cumulus). The $150–250 retail tier is the highest-margin, highest-loyalty zone.

**Tariff macro (2026 primary variable)**: U.S. tariff escalation on Vietnam/China-sourced footwear is the single most material near-term variable for ONON and the broader footwear sector. Nike (35–40% Vietnam sourcing), Adidas (similar), Hoka (Vietnam-heavy) all face identical structural risk. ONON has attempted to diversify but remains substantially Asia-sourced. The 2026 guidance assumes 20% tariff; actual tariff levels and any exemptions or pauses remain uncertain. Tariff escalation could result in industry-wide price increases — which ONON's brand premium supports better than commodity-positioned peers.

**Swiss franc**: CHF has been structurally strong vs. USD (Swiss National Bank policies + safe-haven flows). ONON reports in CHF; ≈62% of revenue is USD-denominated (Americas). CHF strength is a persistent structural headwind to reported revenue growth relative to operational performance. FX risk is specifically tied to the USD/CHF rate — not a commodity or interest rate risk.

**Consumer discretionary environment**: Premium footwear has historically shown relative resilience in mild recessions (aspirational consumers protect premium purchases) but is not immune to severe downturns. ONON's price points ($150–250) are discretionary luxury in emerging markets but approaching commodity in affluent U.S./EU consumer segments. The brand's aspirational positioning creates some downside protection.

**Athleisure secular trend**: The work-from-anywhere + health-conscious lifestyle trend that accelerated in 2020–2023 is a durable tailwind, but growth is decelerating from peak levels. ONON's expansion into lifestyle (Cloudaway, Cloudnova) and apparel aims to capture the athleisure spending wallet as running-specific growth moderates.

## Section 10 — BAIT Framework

### B — Behavioral: **Strong**

A 22% single-day drawdown on a quarter that set a **record gross margin** and *raised* the margin guide is close to a pure behavioral event. The market priced a headline revenue miss and a guide cut while discarding the two disclosures that determine terminal value — margin direction and DTC mix. Eleven analyst price-target cuts followed with zero raises, and the stock made a fresh 52-week low. Then two co-founders bought $4.0M at $30.67, 16% below their own May purchases. Insiders averaging down into a guidance cut they personally authored, against a market selling the headline, is the setup this lens exists to identify.

### A — Analytical: **Moderate-Strong** (up from Moderate)

The differentiated view is that Q2's wholesale contraction was a **deliberate margin trade**, not demand destruction, and the discriminating evidence is public and unambiguous in three places: gross margin *rose* 390bps to a record while wholesale halved; DTC — the channel On fully controls — accelerated to +34.3% c-c and a record 45.7% mix; and the weakness is contained to the Americas while APAC ran +54.7% c-c. A brand losing pricing power cannot produce that combination. Consensus is modelling the print as growth impairment; this page models it as a channel decision with a testable payoff.

The lens is not scored Strong because one disclosed number genuinely contradicts the thesis: **inventory +31% YoY against +13.5% reported sales growth**. That is not what a fully controlled channel looks like. **Falsifier**: if Q3 gross margin prints below 64% or inventory growth remains 25%+ above sales growth, the discipline reading is wrong, this lens drops to Weak, and §13's verbs move to Reduce.

### I — Informational: **Moderate-Strong** (up from Moderate)

The August 14 insider cluster is high-quality, low-latency information: two founders, discretionary open-market purchases (not 10b5-1), at a lower price than their May buys, three days after a print they delivered themselves. They know whether the inventory build is pre-positioning or product not moving, and they bought. Against that, the informational edge decays quickly — the Form 4s are public and were covered within days — and the founders' own conviction is not evidence they are right.

### T — Technical: **Weak**

- Fresh 52-week low of **$28.55**; the entire post-Q1 rally from $36 → $51 has round-tripped and broken through, and the drift has continued on no news
- Below both founder purchase prices ($36.64 May, $30.67 August) — the August level is the only remaining anchor and it is barely holding
- Short interest 8.81% pre-print; post-print positioning unknown until the August 15 settlement publishes
- No buyback, no mechanical bid, and nothing hard until Q3 in ~November

### BAIT Verdict

**Triple overlap (B Strong + A Moderate-Strong + I Moderate-Strong; T Weak)** — up from Double. **Conviction: Moderate-High** (up from Moderate), explicitly conditional. The upgrade rests on a specific, falsifiable claim: that record gross margin plus record DTC mix plus contained geographic weakness together outweigh a revenue miss caused by a channel decision management chose to make. If Q3 does not hold gross margin ≥65% with converging inventory, this verdict reverts to Double and conviction to Moderate — the same quarter that would confirm the thesis is the one that would break it.

## Section 13 — Recommendation & Bottom Line

**Thesis in one sentence**: On cut its own US wholesale shipments rather than discount into a promotional market, and posted a **record 65.4% gross margin** with a *raised* margin guide in the same release that cut its revenue guide — the market sold that 22% as demand destruction, but the top-100 run-specialty partners whose shipments were cut **doubled their Spring/Summer 2027 Cloudsurfer 3 orders**, two co-founders bought $4.0M three days later at 16% below their May purchases, and the whole thesis now reduces to one Q3 test: does gross margin hold ≥65% while a 31% inventory build normalizes?

**For a non-holder**: 🟢 **Initiate** — at $29.10 the stock trades at ≈10.4× FY26E EBITDA, below its entire post-IPO range and at a widening discount to a slower, lower-margin DECK. R/R ≈13.6:1, with the sharp caveat (§12) that the ratio improved only because spot fell to within 14% of the bear case. Spot is now **below both founder purchase prices** ($36.64 May, $30.67 August). Size for the possibility that Q3 goes the other way.

**For a current holder**: 🟢 **Add** — incremental adds in the **$28–36 zone** are favored; spot sits inside it and below both founder marks. The margin evidence and the partner order book (§3) support adding; the inventory number argues for keeping capacity until Q3 resolves it.

🎯 **Attractive entry zone**: **$28 – $42** — a 25% margin of safety to the $55.50 PW EV; spot sits near the **bottom** of the range and below both founder purchase prices. ⚠️ Note the range is nearly exhausted to the downside: below $28 the stock is trading at the bear case, which is a re-underwrite, not a bargain.

🎯 **Trim zone**: **$56 – $85** — at or above PW EV, where the Q2 decision is priced as vindicated but the Bull case is not yet proved.

🔴 **Exit / avoid zone**: **>$85** — Bull-case territory; requires the 2027 cycle, apparel, APAC and margin durability all confirmed.

**Thesis-break triggers** ⚠️ (would force re-rating, possibly to Reduce / Avoid):
- ⚠️ **Gross margin below 64% in any quarter** — tightened from 60%. The entire discipline thesis rests on margin holding while volume was sacrificed; if margin goes too, the sacrifice bought nothing
- ⚠️ **Inventory growth remains 25%+ above sales growth at Q3** — would mean the build was product not moving, not pre-positioning
- ⚠️ Any resumption of structural discounting or a stated move away from the $160/$200/$300 price architecture
- ⚠️ Constant-currency growth falls below 15% for two consecutive quarters (loosened from 18% to reflect the deliberate FY2026 sell-in reduction; below 15% would exceed what the wholesale action alone explains)
- ⚠️ DTC growth decelerates below 20% c-c — the channel On controls is the cleanest read on brand health
- ⚠️ The 2027 franchises (Cloudsurfer 3, Cloudsurfer Max 2, Cloudflow) launch without re-accelerating everyday running
- ⚠️ **The doubled SS27 order book is revised down, or Q3 discloses wholesale order cancellations** — the partner-side evidence in §3 is the load-bearing support for the discipline reading; if it reverses, the demand reading wins
- ⚠️ **Coppetti's 14-month franchise-refresh commitment slips** — a dated, on-record promise made in Q&A (§4), and the cleanest falsifier management has given
- ⚠️ Short interest crosses 15% of float with deteriorating fundamentals
- ⚠️ U.S. tariff rate exceeds 35% without offsetting price execution
- ⚠️ Founder selling, or the absence of further buying on any subsequent decline
