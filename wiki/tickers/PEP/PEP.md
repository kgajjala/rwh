## The Call

PepsiCo is deliberately trading price for volume, and Q2 is the first clean look at the exchange rate. Global organic volume is running at **its highest rate since 2022**; in North America the convenient foods business **gained volume market share**. That is the Elliott playbook — cut SKUs, reset price points, buy the shelf back — working as designed.

The cost is visible in the same release. NA convenient foods **net revenue declined**, *"primarily reflect[ing] lower effective net pricing."* Core operating margin fell **40 bps to 16.8%**. Core constant-currency EPS grew just **1%** against a full-year guide of **+4–6%**, and management conceded North America was *"softer than we anticipated… a more gradual improvement for the balance of this year."* Reported growth flatters this: of the +6.4% revenue, only 2.4 points were organic — FX added 2.2 and acquisitions 1.8.

At $141 you pay **~16.7× forward core earnings for a 4.23% yield** from a wide-moat Dividend King near the bottom of its 52-week range. The setup is unchanged; the error bars around it are wider.

## What I'd Have To Be Wrong About

1. **That the volume-for-price trade is accretive, not dilutive.** Test: Q3 on **Oct 8** — NA convenient foods net revenue. Share gains that keep arriving alongside *declining* segment revenue are a share-buying programme, not a turnaround. One more quarter of that pattern and the productivity offset is failing.
2. **That the back-half acceleration is real.** YTD core cc EPS is +3% against a +4–6% full-year guide, so H2 must deliver roughly +6–8%. Test: whether the guide is *reaffirmed* on Oct 8 or quietly trimmed to the low end. A cut here is worse than a miss — it says the productivity programme is funding price rather than margin.
3. **That the multiple is cheap rather than correctly de-rated.** Consensus moved from Buy to **Hold** in this window and Citi cut to Neutral. If GLP-1 and private label are structurally impairing salty snacks, 16.7× is not a discount — it is the new fair multiple, and the 4.23% yield is the whole return.

## 1. Business & Numbers

Two global businesses — convenient foods (Frito-Lay, Quaker) and beverages (Pepsi, Gatorade, and the 2025 acquisitions) — run across North America and four international segments. The structural asset is the only simultaneous **#1 position in US salty snacks and a top-two position in US beverages**, plus a direct-store-delivery network no snack competitor can replicate.

## 2. Moat & Competition

**Wide.** Three reinforcing sources: brand equity across a portfolio where several individual brands exceed $1B in sales; a **direct-store-delivery network** that controls shelf placement and restock frequency in a category where distribution *is* the barrier; and the snacks-plus-beverages combination, which gives PepsiCo negotiating leverage with retailers that neither a pure snack nor a pure beverage company holds.

The moat is not in doubt. What Q2 tests is its *pricing* expression — a wide moat that has to discount to hold volume is still wide, but it converts to less profit per unit of share.

| Competitor | Position | Threat |
|---|---|---|
| **Private label / value snacks** | Structurally advantaged in trade-down | The reason net pricing turned negative in NA convenient foods. PepsiCo's answer — affordability packs plus SKU rationalisation — concedes price to defend volume. |
| **Coca-Cola** | Larger pure-play beverage, stronger international system | Beats PepsiCo in beverages standalone; PepsiCo's counter is the snack attachment, not head-on cola share. |
| **Mondelēz** | Global snacking scale, sweet-side weighted | Adjacent rather than head-on; competes for the same shelf and the same trade-down consumer. |
| **Celsius / functional upstarts** | Fast-growing energy and functional niches | Where category growth actually is. PepsiCo's response is portfolio evolution — portion control, protein, fibre, hydration, zero sugar — which Laguarta made the centrepiece of the quarter. See [CELH](../CELH/CELH.md). |

**Industry**: developed-market food and beverage volumes are structurally flat, so the group's earnings algorithm runs on price/mix and productivity. GLP-1 adoption is the live structural question; prior primary-source work on this page found aggregate category impact (~1–2%) far below the individual-user effect (20–30%), and nothing in Q2 contradicts that — the NA weakness is priced, not consumed away.

## 3. Management & Capital

**Ramon Laguarta** remains Chairman and CEO, now executing a restructuring he did not originate. The December 2025 agreement with **Elliott Investment Management** (a ~$4B stake) committed PepsiCo to cutting roughly 20% of US SKUs, resetting price points on core brands, closing plants, and a North America supply-chain review reporting in late 2026. Q2 is the first quarter that plainly shows both halves of that bargain: share regained, pricing surrendered.

💰 **Outsider grade: Steward (not Outsider)** — 54 consecutive annual dividend increases and a roughly 7:1 dividends-to-buybacks ratio make PepsiCo a Dividend King by design, not an opportunistic allocator. The Elliott-driven productivity programme is genuine capital discipline, but it was externally imposed rather than self-initiated, which is the opposite of the Thorndike pattern. No material capital-allocation event occurred in this window.

**Appearances (Jul 1 – Sep 2)** — [one: the Q2 earnings call](../../../raw/PEP/appearances/2026-07-09_Q2-2026-earnings-call_laguarta.txt) (Jul 9). No broker-conference fireside, earnings-day television interview or podcast located. Corroborating the quiet window: **PepsiCo filed no 8-K at all between July 9 and September 2.** Laguarta's framing was volume-forward — *"global organic volume has increased at the highest rate since 2022"* — with the margin cost left to the release body rather than the quote.
