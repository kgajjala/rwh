## The Call

PepsiCo has fallen to a new 52-week low, 27% off its high, with no company news since July. The market is voting on the affordability reset before the numbers arrive, and it is voting for the Bear. Holding our Bull-to-Base mix fixed, today's price earns an ordinary 8% return only if the Bear (earnings stall, then inflation-only growth) is **68% likely**. We assign 28%, because the evidence so far runs the other way: volume share is coming back in North American snacks, and the weakest branded rival is retreating. Campbell's cut its dividend 36% after a 6% organic decline in snacks (§3).

What the market has right is the owner-earnings arithmetic. The forward dividend absorbs **99% of five-year average owner earnings**, three years of new capital earned a negative return, and net debt rose $7.2B over five years (§1). The yield is not a cushion that compounds; any growth has to come from price and the Elliott margin programme, not reinvestment.

That makes this an Initiate on the numbers, 13.3% a year against the hurdle, but a **probability-fragile** one: it clears only while the Bear stays below **30%**, two points above where we put it. Two of the three kill criteria resolve at 6 a.m. on Oct 8, and each failure pre-commits the verb back to Watch. The number that settles it is **PepsiCo Foods North America net revenue**. Flat or up alongside rising volume share means the price investment is buying the franchise back; another decline means it is only buying share.

## What I'd Have To Be Wrong About

1. **That the volume-for-price trade is accretive.** Test: PepsiCo Foods North America Q3 net revenue at or above last year, with volume share holding or rising, in the Q3 release on **Oct 8**. **If Fail →** move 10 points from Base to Bear (PW EV $181, 12.2%/yr) → **Watch**.
2. **That the back-half acceleration is real.** Test: the FY2026 guide of +4% to +6% core constant-currency EPS is reaffirmed on **Oct 8**, not cut to the low end or below. **If Fail →** Base EPS growth falls to 4% a year (Base $196, PW EV $185, 12.7%/yr) → **Watch**.
3. **That incremental returns recover.** Test: in the FY2026 10-K (**February 2027**), core operating margin is up year over year, capex is under 5% of sales, and owner earnings cover the dividend. **If Fail →** move 7 points from Bull to Bear (PW EV $180, 12.2%/yr) → **Watch**, and the stage stays mature ex-growth.

## 3. Moat, Customer & Competition

| Read | Call | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | **Branding** + **scale economies** | Several $1B+ brands; a direct-store-delivery network that controls shelf placement, where distribution *is* the barrier. ROIC of 15–19% against an 8% cost of capital puts EPV ÷ invested capital at ≈1.8–2.4: a franchise |
| Trend | **Narrowing** | Pricing power, the test that matters for a staple: FY2025 took +3.2% price and lost 1.5% of volume. 2026 gives price back to win the volume home |
| Profit pool | Shifting to retailers and private label in the trade-down | DSD is the counterweight to buyer power; snacks plus beverages gives negotiating leverage neither a pure snack nor a pure beverage company holds |
| Customer | Households buying affordable indulgence many times a week | Retention proxy is volume share: lost in FY2025, recovering in 2026 at a price. Repeat, CAC and LTV are n/d |

**Wide, narrowing**: the moat is not in question; its conversion into pricing is. **Scale economies, shared or harvested?** FY2022–25 harvested them, taking price and losing volume. 2026 shares them, cutting price and funding it with productivity. That is the right direction for the moat, but with core margin −15 bps year to date the price cut is running ahead of the savings, so the jury is out.
