**Breaks if**: the price investment in North American snacks never earns back margin. That means PepsiCo Foods North America revenue stays flat to down while volume share rises, and core margin keeps falling through 2027.

## The Call

The Oct 8 print failed both tests written the day before. Core constant-currency EPS guidance fell to **+1–2%** from the low end of +4–6%. PepsiCo Foods North America (PFNA) net revenue slipped 0.3% even though volume share rose. Each failure was pre-committed to Watch, and the rebuilt case agrees: **PW EV $175 returns 11.3% a year**, below the hurdle.

The market took the cut calmly; the stock rose 3.7% on the day because it had already priced worse. Holding our Bull-to-Base mix fixed, $127 earns an ordinary 8% return only if the Bear is **63% likely**. We now assign 38%, up from 28%. That gap is not yet an opportunity, because the evidence behind it moved the wrong way:
- the price cuts are buying volume (+0.5% in PFNA) but not revenue;
- PFNA core operating profit fell 12% in constant currency;
- Q3's 3% core profit growth needed 4 points of tariff refunds.

Break-even Bear odds are 23%, so the case clears 13% again only if the Bear falls 15 points, or the price falls to $117. The number that settles it is the **FY2027 guide in February**. Mid-single-digit core constant-currency EPS growth, funded by the new structural cost cuts, would say the reset has turned. Another low-single-digit year would say the franchise is paying for its volume indefinitely.

## What I'd Have To Be Wrong About

1. **That the new cost programme is real money.** Test: by the Q4 release (**early February 2027**), PepsiCo quantifies the structural cost programme and the Elliott-committed North America supply-chain review, either as ≥$1B of savings or as a reaffirmed ≥100 bps 2026–28 core margin target. **If Fail →** move 7 points from Bull to Base (PW EV $172, 10.8%/yr) → **Watch** (entry ≤$115).
2. **That 2026 is the trough year for earnings growth.** Test: FY2027 guidance in the Q4 release (**February 2027**) calls for at least +4% core constant-currency EPS. **If Fail →** Base EPS growth falls to 3% a year (Base $185, PW EV $172, 10.8%/yr) → **Watch** (entry ≤$115).
3. **That incremental returns recover.** Test: in the FY2026 10-K (**February 2027**), core operating margin is up year over year, capex is under 5% of sales, and owner earnings cover the dividend. ⚠️ This test is tracking to fail: core margin is −25 bps year to date, and the Q4 North America margin is guided down. **If Fail →** move 7 points from Bull to Bear (PW EV $166, 10.1%/yr) → **Watch** (entry ≤$111), and the stage stays mature ex-growth.

## 3. Moat, Customer & Competition

| Read | Call | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | **Branding** + **scale economies** | Several $1B+ brands; a direct-store-delivery network that controls shelf placement, where distribution *is* the barrier. ROIC of 15–19% against an 8% cost of capital puts EPV ÷ invested capital at ≈1.8–2.4: a franchise |
| Trend | **Narrowing** | Pricing power, the test that matters for a staple: FY2025 took +3.2% price and lost 1.5% of volume. 2026 gives price back (PFNA −1%) and wins volume, but not revenue |
| Profit pool | Shifting to retailers and private label in the trade-down | DSD is the counterweight to buyer power; snacks plus beverages gives negotiating leverage neither a pure snack nor a pure beverage company holds |
| Customer | Households buying affordable indulgence many times a week | Retention proxy is volume share: lost in FY2025, recovering in 2026 at a price. Repeat, CAC and LTV are n/d |

**Wide, narrowing**: the moat is not in question; its conversion into pricing is. **Scale economies, shared or harvested?** FY2022–25 harvested them, taking price and losing volume. 2026 shares them, cutting price and funding it with productivity. Q3 answered the open question: the price cut is running ahead of the savings (§1). Sharing is the right direction for the moat, but it is costing earnings now.

**Scuttlebutt**: Campbell's results cut both ways. But the US salty-snack dollar pool is shrinking, which supports the Bear. Q3 is consistent with the second reading. **Industry**: GLP-1 adoption is the live structural question. Earlier primary-source work found the category-wide impact (~1–2%) far below the effect on an individual user, and nothing since contradicts that.

## 4. Management & Capital

**Ramon Laguarta** (Chairman and CEO) is executing a restructuring he did not originate. The December 2025 **Elliott** agreement committed PepsiCo to a ~20% US SKU cut, plant closures, reset price points and a North America supply-chain review by late 2026 ([agreement](https://www.pepsico.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025/pepsico-announces-priorities-to-enhance-shareholder-value-and-provides-preliminary-2026-outlook)).

Elliott took no board seats, and its 13F position has been flat at 1,275,000 shares for four quarters. The commitments are therefore reputational, not structural ([13F history](../../../raw/PEP/filings/2026-09-22_Elliott-13F-position-history.txt)). CFO **Steve Schmitt** (ex-Walmart U.S.) was hired for cost discipline. Joaquin Duato (Johnson & Johnson) joins the board on Dec 1 ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/77476/000007747626000042/pep-20260917.htm)).

**Incentives**: CEO target pay is ~27% salary, ~23% annual incentive and ~50% long-term equity ([2026 proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/77476/000130817926000169/pep015012_def14a.htm)); the metric weightings could not be parsed `[link pending]`.
