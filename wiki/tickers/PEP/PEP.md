**A wide-moat Dividend King at a 4.5% yield whose last three years of new capital earned nothing and whose organic growth was all price until this year; a further 5% fall to a new 52-week low lifts the probability-weighted case to 12.4% a year — closer to the hurdle, still short of it, and still on the wrong side of a back-half-loaded guide.**

**Breaks if**: full-year core constant-currency EPS lands below the +4% guidance floor. Q2 printed +1% and YTD is +3%, so the guide is back-half loaded; that is the number carrying the thesis.

## The Call

PepsiCo is deliberately trading price for volume, and Q2 was the first clean look at the exchange rate. Global organic volume is running at **its highest rate since 2022**; in North America the convenient foods business **gained volume market share**. That is the Elliott playbook working as designed: cut SKUs, reset price points, buy the shelf back.

The cost is visible in the same release. NA convenient foods **net revenue declined** on lower net pricing, core operating margin fell **40 bps to 16.8%**, and core constant-currency EPS grew **1%** against a full-year guide of +4–6%.

What the price does not reflect is how little the capital has been earning. Between FY2022 and FY2025 PepsiCo added **$11.6B of invested capital** and after-tax operating profit *fell* $0.3B; even on core profit the incremental return is single digits (§1). A staple that cannot earn on new money is a yield instrument: at $138 the yield plus 5% core EPS growth compounds at **9.3%**, re-rating supplies the rest of the Base. The market still prices about **4% perpetual growth** into the shares, more than PepsiCo has organically delivered in either of the last two years. Quality business, unrewarding price, not yet low enough.

## What I'd Have To Be Wrong About

1. **That the volume-for-price trade is accretive, not dilutive.** Test: Q3 on **Oct 8**: NA convenient foods net revenue. Share gains that keep arriving alongside *declining* segment revenue are a share-buying programme, not a turnaround.
2. **That the back-half acceleration is real.** YTD core cc EPS is +3% against +4–6%, so H2 must deliver roughly +6–8%. Test: whether the guide is *reaffirmed* on Oct 8 or trimmed to the low end. A cut says the productivity programme is funding price rather than margin.
3. **That incremental returns recover as the Elliott programme lands.** Test: the FY2026 10-K in February 2027. If core operating margin has not expanded year over year with capex held under 5% of sales, the ≥100 bps 2026–28 commitment is already behind and the ex-growth classification stands.

## 1. Business & Numbers

Two global businesses, convenient foods (Frito-Lay, Quaker) and beverages (Pepsi, Gatorade, the 2025 acquisitions), run across North America and four international segments. The structural asset is the only simultaneous **#1 in US salty snacks and top-two in US beverages**, plus a direct-store-delivery network no snack competitor can replicate.

| Lever | Contribution now | Runway | Evidence | Management intent | Stage |

The Base case rests on **margin (productivity offsetting the price investment) and international volume**; the Bull needs the NA volume recovery to stick *and* margin to expand past 17.5%. Every lever is core-adjacent; the M&A is the one that has yet to earn its capital.

## 3. Moat, Customer & Competition

| Read | Call | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | **Branding** + **scale economies** | Several $1B+ brands; a direct-store-delivery network that controls shelf placement and restock where distribution *is* the barrier |
| Trend | **Narrowing** | On the test that matters for a staple, pricing power: FY2025 took +3.2% price and lost 1.5% of volume; 2026 gives price back to win the volume home |
| Profit pool | Shifting to retailers and private label in the trade-down | DSD is the counterweight to buyer power; snacks-plus-beverages gives negotiating leverage neither a pure snack nor a pure beverage company holds |
| Customer | Households buying affordable indulgence and convenience many times a week | Retention proxy is volume share, lost in FY2025 and recovering in 2026 at a price; the last increase cost volume, the current move is a cut; repeat, CAC, LTV n/d |

**Wide, narrowing**: the moat is not in question, its conversion into pricing is.

| Competitor | Position | Threat |
|---|---|---|
| **Private label / value snacks** | Structurally advantaged in trade-down | The reason net pricing turned negative in NA convenient foods. |
| **Coca-Cola** | Larger pure-play beverage, stronger international system | Beats PepsiCo in beverages standalone; PepsiCo's counter is the snack attachment. |
| **Mondelēz** | Global snacking scale, sweet-side weighted | Adjacent rather than head-on; same shelf, same trade-down consumer. |
| **Celsius / functional upstarts** | Fast-growing energy and functional niches | Where category growth actually is. See [CELH](../CELH/CELH.md). |

**Industry**: GLP-1 adoption is the live structural question; prior primary-source work on this page found aggregate category impact (~1–2%) far below the individual-user effect, and nothing in Q2 contradicts that.

## 4. Management & Capital

**Ramon Laguarta** remains Chairman and CEO, executing a restructuring he did not originate. The December 2025 agreement with **Elliott Investment Management** (a ~$4B stake) committed PepsiCo to cutting roughly 20% of US SKUs, closing plants, resetting price points, and a North America supply-chain review reporting in late 2026. **Steve Schmitt** (CFO since November 2025, ex-Walmart U.S.) was hired for supply-chain and cost discipline.

**Incentives**: CEO target pay is ~27% salary, ~23% annual incentive, ~50% long-term equity ([2026 proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/77476/000130817926000169/pep015012_def14a.htm)); the metric weightings in the CD&A could not be parsed from any available copy `[link pending]`, a gap logged in `index.md`. **Guidance grade: Mixed, beats the quarter and misses the year**: core EPS beat consensus in seven of eight quarters, each by $0.01–0.09, while FY2025 delivered +1.7% organic and flat core cc EPS against a guide of low-single-digit and mid-single-digit ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/stocks/NASDAQ/PEP/earnings/)).

**Promises ledger**

|---|---|---|
| Long-term algorithm: 4–6% organic revenue, high-single-digit core cc EPS | CAGNY, 2022–23 | 🔴 FY2024 ~+2% / +9%; FY2025 +1.7% / 0% |
| FY2025: low-single-digit organic, mid-single-digit core cc EPS | Q4 2024 release, Feb 2025 | 🔴 +1.7% / 0% |
| ≥100 bps core operating margin expansion 2026–28; 20% SKU cut; NA supply-chain review by late 2026 | [Elliott agreement](https://www.pepsico.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025/pepsico-announces-priorities-to-enhance-shareholder-value-and-provides-preliminary-2026-outlook), Dec 2025 | 🟡 Nine months into a 36-month window and core margin is **−15 bps**, not up; the review is now due |

💰 **Outsider grade: Steward (not Outsider)**: 54 consecutive dividend increases and a ~7:1 dividends-to-buybacks ratio make PepsiCo a Dividend King by design, not an opportunistic allocator. The productivity programme is genuine capital discipline, externally imposed. No capital-allocation event this window — and none at the low, which is itself the point: the stock fell 5% to a new 52-week low and the buyback stayed at its programmatic ~$1B/yr pace.

**Board**: on Sep 17 PepsiCo elected **Joaquin Duato** — chairman and CEO of Johnson & Johnson — an independent director effective Dec 1, joining the Audit Committee ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/77476/000007747626000042/pep-20260917.htm)). A serious operator of a large, regulated, brand-led business. ⚠️ The filing makes **no reference to Elliott or to any designation right**: this is ordinary board refresh, not an activist seat.

**Activist engagement — what the filings actually show.** The margin commitments this thesis leans on were externally imposed, so how much force still sits behind them matters. [Elliott's 13F history](../../../raw/PEP/filings/2026-09-22_Elliott-13F-position-history.txt), parsed from EDGAR:

| Period end | Elliott disclosed PEP position |
|---|---|
| Sep 30 2025 (stake reported) | 1,275,000 sh · $179.1M |
| Dec 31 2025 (settlement) | 1,275,000 sh · $183.0M |
| Mar 31 2026 | 1,275,000 sh · $198.0M |
| Jun 30 2026 (latest) | 1,275,000 sh · **$172.6M** |

Three things follow. **The position has not moved in four quarters** — no add, no trim, so there is no exit signal and no escalation signal. **It is ~$167M at today's price, not $4B**: the reported stake is ~96% held outside 13F reporting, almost certainly in total-return swaps, so the economic exposure is real but unverifiable. And the enforcement is **reputational, not structural** — Elliott took no board seats in the December settlement, has never filed a 13D on PEP, and no proxy-contest material has ever been filed. Steinberg's stated posture remains *"continued engagement with the Company."*

The practical read: the commitments are public, dated and reputationally binding on management, but nobody with a board seat is holding the pen. 📅 The supply-chain review promised for **late 2026** is the first hard test of whether that is enough.

**Appearances (Jul 1 – Sep 22)**: one, the [Q2 call](../../../raw/PEP/appearances/2026-07-09_Q2-2026-earnings-call_laguarta.txt) (Jul 9), framed volume-forward with the margin cost left to the release body. No broker-conference fireside, television interview or podcast located in the extended window either; the IR events page lists nothing before Oct 8. Management has said nothing in public for eleven weeks while the stock made a new low — an absence worth naming, not an inference.
