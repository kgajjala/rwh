## The Call

FY2026 was carried entirely by price. Organic sales grew 1% *"from higher pricing"* with volume and mix unchanged for the full year, and Q4 was the tell: net sales +2% (all FX and rounding), **organic 0%, volume 0%, core EPS −3%, currency-neutral core EPS −5%**. Q3's +2% volume — the single datapoint that justified the recovery thesis on this page — did not repeat.

FY2027 does not fix it. Core EPS is guided to **in-line-to-+3%** ($6.89–$7.11, midpoint $7.00) against roughly **$1B after-tax** of raw-material, energy and freight inflation plus interest and FX — management sizes the combined hit at **$0.56 per share, an 8% drag**. Adjusted free-cash-flow productivity is guided down from 100% to **85–90%**. Operating income already *fell* 4% in FY2026 ($20.5B → $19.7B) on 3% higher sales.

At $147 that is ~21× forward core earnings for a business guiding ~1.5% growth. The dividend is impregnable — a 70th consecutive raise, $10.2B paid against $19.6B of operating cash flow. You are simply paying a premium multiple for an algorithm that has stopped delivering.

## What I'd Have To Be Wrong About

1. **That the cost drag is transient, not structural.** Test: Q2 FY2027 gross margin (Jan 2027). Gross margin has now compressed for seven-plus quarters; if commodities anniversary and productivity offsets as management claims, compression should stop there. If it doesn't, the $0.56 headwind is a new baseline.
2. **That volume returns without buying it.** Test: Q1 FY2027 organic volume on **Oct 22**. Pricing supplied 100% of FY2026 organic growth. Another 0% volume quarter says the algorithm is broken rather than paused — and PG has no price headroom left with the consumer trading down.
3. **That 21× survives ~1.5% growth.** Staples multiples are compressing across the group. The market is paying PG a premium for safety; that premium, not the earnings, is the larger part of what is at risk here.

Ten product categories across five reportable segments, sold in ~180 countries. ## 2. Moat & Competition

**Wide**, and not in question — brand equity across ten categories, a distribution and retail-shelf position no challenger can replicate, and R&D scale that funds continuous product superiority. What *is* in question is whether a wide moat still converts into pricing power when the consumer trades down.

PG's own disclosure is the sharpest available evidence, and it is mixed: **26 of the top 50 category/country combinations held or grew share**, but only **5 of 10 product categories held or grew share globally**, with aggregate value and volume share *"exiting the year roughly flat."* Holding share is not the same as compounding it.

| Competitor | Position | Threat |
|---|---|---|
| **Private label** | Structurally advantaged in a trade-down cycle | The real competitor now. PG's answer is product superiority at a price premium; Q4's flat volume against +1% pricing is what that tension looks like in the numbers. |
| **Unilever** | Global scale peer, overlapping in personal and home care | Comparable breadth; currently running its own portfolio reshaping, so competing more on focus than on price. |
| **Colgate-Palmolive** | Narrower, deeper — oral care and home care | Higher-margin, more concentrated. Wins where category focus beats conglomerate breadth. |
| **Kimberly-Clark** | Direct in Baby, Feminine & Family Care | Head-on in PG's weakest FY2026 segment (organic −2%), which is where private label pressure also lands hardest. |

**Industry**: developed-market staples volumes are structurally low-growth, so the category's earnings algorithm has depended on price/mix for years. FY2026 is the year that stopped working for PG — pricing delivered all the organic growth and volume delivered none.

## 3. Management & Capital

**Shailesh Jejurikar** became CEO in 2025 and has now added the **Chairman** title, consolidating the role Jon Moeller held; **Andre Schulten** remains CFO. FY2026 is the first full year owned by this team, which makes the FY2027 guide their first real statement of the algorithm they intend to run.

💰 **Outsider grade: Steward (not Outsider)** — the archetype for the category. 70 consecutive annual dividend increases and a 136th consecutive year of payment; >$15B returned in FY2026 ($10.2B dividends, $5.0B buybacks) with roughly the same planned for FY2027. The buyback is a fixed ~$5B/year program, not an opportunistic one — it runs at the same pace whether the stock is at $167 or $138, which is precisely the behaviour Thorndike's test excludes. Nothing here is mismanaged; it is simply not capital allocation as a source of alpha.

**Primary sources.** The [FY2026 letter](../../../raw/PG/shareholder-letters/2026_letter.txt) (Pattern C, filed Aug 28) is notable for what it concedes. Jejurikar leads with *"We met our core objectives for fiscal year 2026 despite unexpected headwinds"* and states plainly that *"on a currency neutral basis, core earnings per share were in-line with the prior year"* — zero growth, said out loud. The bull evidence he offers is sequencing: top line *"moving from up one point in the first half of the year to two points in the second half,"* share trends improving in the back half. He closes with *"there is clearly more work to be done to sustain and accelerate the momentum underway."* Read against a Q4 that printed 0% organic, the second-half acceleration claim rests on Q3, not Q4.

**Appearances (May 30 – Sep 2)** — [one: the Q4 FY2026 call](../../../raw/PG/appearances/2026-07-29_Q4-FY26-earnings-call_jejurikar-schulten.txt) (Jul 29). No broker-conference fireside, earnings-day television interview or podcast located in the window. 📅 The Barclays Global Consumer Staples Conference runs in early-to-mid September; PG has presented in each prior year but **2026 participation was not announced as of Sep 2** — expected, not confirmed.
