## The Call

The market has priced Uber as an ex-growth utility: a **6.3% free-cash-flow yield** that at a 9% discount rate implies **1–3% growth forever**. The business is doing 22%. Gross bookings hit $58.0B in Q2, above the top of guidance for a fourth straight quarter, on record users (208M, +16%), frequency and drivers; Adjusted EBITDA rose 33% to $2.8B and trailing free cash flow crossed **$10B**.

The number that proves the setup is invested capital: **~$12.1B at the end of 2025, ~$12.0B at the end of 2023**, while after-tax operating profit rose from $0.9B to $4.4B. Growth has cost nothing. That is a network business at scale, and it is why a Base case with no multiple expansion still compounds earnings at high teens.

What the market is right to worry about is the pivot. In one quarter Uber put ~$4B into a Delivery Hero stake, agreed to pay €41.50 a share ($13.7B net) for the rest with a €14B bridge, committed "over $10 billion" to autonomy, sold ~$950M of Aurora stock, and bought back $510M against $3.0B the quarter before. Together they turn a capital-free compounder into an acquirer; the return on the next $25B is the only open question.

## What I'd Have To Be Wrong About

1. **That the buyback pause is temporary.** Test: Q3 (Nov 3) and Q4 (Feb 2027) cash-flow statements. Repurchases below ~$1B in Q3 and not back above ~$1.5B in Q4, with $16B authorized and the stock under $96, means the capital framework changed and management did not say so.
2. **That autonomy accrues to the marketplace, not the vehicle owner.** Test: 15 AV cities live on Uber by Dec 31, 2026 (7 at Q2) and category share in Los Angeles, San Francisco and Phoenix still up year over year at the Q4 print. A Waymo standalone-app share gain in Austin or Atlanta, or a missed city count, is the disconfirmation.
3. **That the trips engine is intact under the accounting noise.** Test: Q3 trips growth ≥17% and Mobility gross bookings ≥+18% constant currency. Brazil supply softness cost two points in Q2; if trips growth prints below 15% the audience-and-frequency flywheel is slowing, whatever revenue says.

## 3. Moat, Customer & Competition

| Read | Call | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | **Network economies** + **scale economies**, with Uber One as an emerging switching cost | Local two-sided liquidity: 10.2M drivers keep waits short, short waits keep riders, riders keep drivers; scale in insurance, mapping and marketplace technology |
| Trend | **Widening** | Delivery retention at a multi-year high; member share of Delivery bookings +20 pts in two years; category position up in every top-ten international market; share up in the three most mature AV markets |
| Profit pool | Drivers take ≈43% of bookings, Uber ≈5% as EBITDA | Autonomy moves the driver pool to whoever owns the vehicle; the AV spend is a bid to sit between vehicle owner and rider rather than be bypassed |
| Customer | 208M monthly consumers wanting a ride or a meal in minutes without owning the asset | Uber One members spend ~3× (46M at year-end, 50M+ by May); US prices were *cut* with insurance savings and trips grew fastest where prices fell most; CAC n/d, proxy is record first-time users |

**Wide, widening**, with the AV caveat that the moat and the disintermediation risk are the same fact.

| Competitor | Arena | Position | Threat read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Waymo** | Robotaxi, US | 3,000+ driverless cars, ~500K paid rides a week, own app; still on Uber in Austin and Atlanta | The bypass risk made concrete; also Uber's proof that AV operators want its demand |
| **Tesla** | Robotaxi | Cybercab charging fares in Austin ([TSLA](../TSLA/TSLA.md)) | A vertically integrated owner with no reason to share take rate |
| **DoorDash** | US delivery | ~65% US restaurant share ([DASH](../DASH/DASH.md)) | Structural #2 position for Uber Eats in the US; Uber wins internationally |
| **Lyft** | US rides | ~25% US share | Multi-homing drivers; no international or AV story |
| **Delivery Hero** | International delivery | 50 markets, $42B GB | Soon owned; the integration risk sits in §6 |
| **Bolt, InDrive, Didi, Grab** | International rides | Regional leaders; Uber holds Didi and Grab stakes | Lower take rates; Uber competes on breadth and membership |

**Industry**: ride-hail is still a small share of passenger miles; the structural question is whether autonomy is commercialized through marketplaces or through owned fleets with their own apps. Management's Q2 framing, that AVs will look like foundation models, several winners distributed both directly and through platforms, is the bull case stated as an industry thesis.

## 4. Management & Capital

**Dara Khosrowshahi** (CEO since 2017) took Uber from crisis to $10B of free cash flow; **Balaji Krishnamurthy** (CFO since February 2026, internal) has now run two prints. Single-class stock; insider ownership ~1%.

**Incentives** ([2026 proxy](../../../raw/UBER/filings/UBER-DEF14A-2026.htm)): the 2025 cash bonus was 60% on **Gross Bookings, Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA less SBC**, equally weighted, and 40% on five strategic priorities including autonomy; 2026 PRSUs are 90% financial with a **three-year Non-GAAP Operating Income growth** metric replacing annual EBITDA margin, plus a relative-TSR modifier. Nothing is paid on return on capital or per-share value, in the year Uber becomes an acquirer. **Guidance grade: Consistent beater**: bookings above the high end of guidance four quarters running; EPS ahead of consensus in seven of nine ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/stocks/NYSE/UBER/earnings/)).

💰 **[Outsider grade](../../frameworks/outsiders.md): Outsider-leaning, on probation.** The record is genuine: the first buyback (2024) became a $3.0B record quarter into the 52-week low, and the 2020 Aurora sale preserved autonomy optionality without capex.
