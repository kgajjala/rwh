UnitedHealth closed at $412.02 on Monday, November 16. By Wednesday's close it was [[price]], down 15.1% in two sessions on 8 million and then 17 million shares, against about 4 to 5 million on a normal day. Nothing came from the company. The cause was a CMS proposal released on the Monday, and a senator's post on the Wednesday.

### What happened

On November 16 CMS proposed **CRUSH** (CMS-6098-P), a program-integrity rule across Medicare, Medicaid and the exchanges that it says would save "nearly $90 billion over 10 years" ([press release](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-crush-proposed-rule-aims-reap-nearly-90-billion-taxpayers-savings-targeting-fraud-across)). The provision that moved the stock is the largest one. CMS would codify Medicare Advantage RADV audits with extrapolation and no fee-for-service adjuster, starting with payment year 2018 audits, and estimates it would collect about $9 billion per payment year on average ([fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/comprehensive-regulations-uncover-suspicious-healthcare-crush-proposed-rule-cms-6098-p)). It would also require every provider billing an MA plan to enroll in traditional Medicare, and make fraud referrals mandatory. Comments close January 19, 2027.

The stock fell 5.4% on November 17. On November 18 Senator Warren wrote that UnitedHealth, CVS and Cigna "take a cut at every step," two weeks after Democrats won both chambers of Congress, and the stock fell another 10.2% while Humana fell 13% ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/unitedhealth-sinks-12-cms-fraud-194116346.html)).

### Is the move warranted?

Partly. The essay's chapter on the two days ([Two Days in November](#two-days-in-november-how-much-is-fear)) has the full argument; the short version:

- **The bill.** At UnitedHealth's ≈28% share, CRUSH means ≈$2.5 billion pre-tax per payment year, nine back years, ≈$13 a share in present value if all of it is collected, ≈$7 a share at the even odds I give it in court `[Estimate]`. Prospective years are biddable and sit in the margins.
- **The court.** The 2023 version of this rule was vacated in September 2025 for lack of proper notice; the Fifth Circuit heard the government's appeal on October 5 and, by STAT's account, seemed inclined to agree with Humana. CRUSH fixes the notice problem, not the actuarial-equivalence or retroactivity arguments.
- **The group.** Cigna, which owns no MA business, fell 4.9%; Elevance and Centene about 10%. About a third of UnitedHealth's fall was a sector re-rating on politics.
- **My numbers.** Counting the CRUSH line and a five-point shift from Bull to Bear for a Congress and an agency pulling the same way, my value fell by about a third of what the stock lost. About a third is a practical threat to earnings power; two-thirds is fear.
- **But the starting point matters.** At $412 the stock priced a Bear probability of about 2% on my case values. At [[price]] it prices about [[implied_bear]]; mine is 30%. The fall overshot the news and landed about at fair value. It is not yet cheap.

### The last scare: the 2027 rate notice, January to April

This is the second time this year a CMS proposal has knocked a fifth to a sixth off the stock. The first one is a useful guide, but only partly.

| | January 2026: 2027 advance notice | November 2026: CRUSH |
|---|---|---|
| What CMS proposed | A +0.09% rate update against ≈4–6% expected; exclude unlinked chart reviews from risk scores (>$7B a year); recalibrate the risk model | Extrapolated RADV audits back to PY2018, no FFS adjuster, ≈$9B per payment year |
| Kind of hit | Recurring: lower revenue per member from 2027 on | Mostly one-time: a back-bill on revenue already earned |

The lobbying worked, and fast. The Better Medicare Alliance ran research, ads, signature drives and meetings, and credited "thousands of beneficiaries" with the result. TD Cowen read the outcome as proof of the industry's influence over CMS, with an eye on not disrupting seniors before the midterms. Anyone who bought the January panic made about 30% in three months.

The detail that matters now is what CMS gave and what it kept. It gave on the number: the rate went up by 2.4 points, mostly because newer fee-for-service data raised the growth rate, and it put off recalibrating the risk model. It kept the coding crackdown: the chart-review exclusion, the one provision aimed squarely at how UnitedHealth adds diagnoses, was finalized. Medicare director Chris Klomp said CMS was "not throwing the risk adjustment model out." CRUSH is entirely the second kind of provision. It is sold as fraud recovery, it is scored at ≈$90 billion of savings, and it comes from an administrator who has made program integrity his brand.

Three things also cut against a lobbying rescue this time. The midterms are over, so the political cost of squeezing plans before an election is gone. The new Democratic majorities are, if anything, more hostile to MA coding than CMS is. And "protect seniors' benefits" is a harder argument to make for a bill on payments from 2018 to 2026, which no longer fund anyone's benefits.

The relief valve this time is the courts, not lobbying. A rate notice cannot realistically be litigated; RADV extrapolation already has been. The 2023 rule was vacated in 2025, and the Fifth Circuit panel sounded sympathetic to Humana in October. The most likely soft landing is a final rule that phases in, starts later than PY2018 or adds some form of adjuster, plus a court ruling that narrows what CMS can collect for past years. That is why I hold the back years at even odds and do not assume a January-style reversal.

**Is a similar scare in the works? Yes, and it has the same calendar.** In January 2026 a CMS proposal, the Q4 print and the full-year guide landed in the same 48 hours. The next eight weeks stack up the same way: the 2027 outlook (by Dec 31), CRUSH comments closing (Jan 19), the Q4 release (mid-January), the CY2028 advance notice (late January or early February, from a CMS that has just shown its hand), and a Fifth Circuit ruling at any time. A second leg down on the 2028 advance notice is plausible, and it would be a recurring revenue hit, closer to January's than to CRUSH. If it comes, the lesson of 2026 is that the trough of a CMS scare has been the time to look, not to sell. A move to my [[entry]] entry is within the range of what the last scare did ($352 to $259, −26% peak to trough). The final notice in early April 2027 is when the relief, if any, would come.

### What it does to the tests and the verdict

No standing test was resolved: the Q4 print, the 2027 guide and any DOJ action all lie ahead. CRUSH is folded into test 3, which now also requires the Fifth Circuit to rule for Humana, or the final rule to drop the 2018 start. Test 2 now asks that the 2027 guide not carve a RADV reserve out of adjusted earnings.
